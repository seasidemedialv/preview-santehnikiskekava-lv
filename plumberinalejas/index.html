Before the plumber sets off for Alejas in Ķekava parish, it helps to know a few things about the road, the water and the drains. They are set out below, and most of them can be entered in the request form.

What to find out before the trip to Alejas

  1. Which street to drive in by. The main streets of Alejas, Akmeņkaļu Street and Priežu Street, belong to the municipality, but some sections are on private land. If the last stretch to your gate is private or closed off, write it in the form: then we know which side to come from and whether the work needs a neighbour’s consent, for example if a pipe has to be dug in along the street.
  2. How the house gets its water and where its drains end up. Before the work we ask what is in the yard: a private well or borehole, a septic tank, a holding tank or something else. Alejas is not in the Ķekavas nami tariff list, and the answer decides which job comes first if a sink or WC will not drain.
  3. Whether the septic tank or holding tank is registered. In Ķekava Municipality, decentralised systems are registered with the municipality. If the house was bought recently or the system was rebuilt, check that the registration is current and matches what actually stands in the yard.
  4. The old strip along the Daugava or the new part towards Katlakalns. The historic part of Alejas, with compact low-rise buildings along the Daugava, began to form in the 1960s. In 2009 the village boundary was extended as far as Katlakalns, where detached-house building is permitted. An old house may have pipes from different decades; a new house tends to have a design with outlet positions.

1960s houses and new detached houses in one village

Alejas is a medium-sized village with an area of 1.82 km². The number of residents according to Central Statistical Bureau and Official Statistics Portal data was 185 (1989), 379 (2011) and 451 (2021). There are few streets, so in a request the most important thing is the street name, for example Akmeņkaļu or Priežu Street.

In a house built in the 1960s and rebuilt several times since, the water pipes may be a mix of steel and plastic, and the route of the outlets under the floor may not be known. In such a house the replacement starts where the pipe enters the house and moves on to the fixtures; we describe this on the page about replacing pipes in a private house. A run to one fixture (cold water, hot water or waste) costs from €35 per run, materials extra.

If the drains run slowly or back up, the job is drain unblocking. Clearing a drain with a cable (sink, bath, shower or WC) costs from €40 per call-out, and the outdoor drain section from the house to the manhole from €50 per call-out with the first hour. For a newer house with its own well and septic tank, a yearly plumbing check suits, and its price is set by the size of the house and the number of fixtures.

Between the P137, Titurga and Sausā Daugava

Alejas, formerly Dzelzkaļi and in Soviet times the Daugava settlement, lies between the P137 road and Sausā Daugava (the Dry Daugava). Near Alejas the Ostvalds canal flows into the river Titurga, and the Titurga flows into Sausā Daugava. A part of Sausā Daugava lies within the village area, so the boundary of Alejas is also a municipality boundary. Old house papers may carry one of the earlier names, but the address is Alejas, Ķekava parish.

Production and service sites are concentrated along the road between Titurga and the Ostvalds canal. If you mention a landmark in the request, this is the useful one: by the road or by the river, on the Titurga or the canal side.

Septic tank emptying and the receipt to keep at home

The Ķekava Municipality rules on decentralised sewerage provide for a septic tank to be emptied at least once a year, but the paper after the emptying is just as important. The contract or transaction document must show the service address, a description and value of the transaction, the quantity collected with its unit, the date and the service provider’s details, and it must be shown to a municipal representative if he asks for it (point 33.5).

This matters directly for plumbing. If the septic tank is full, the water does not leave the house, and it looks like a blocked pipe. So before cleaning a pipe it is worth knowing when the septic tank was last emptied: a receipt with a date answers that faster than memory.

Ķekava Municipality binding regulations No. 20/2022: point 9 (septic tank at least once a year), point 33.5 (transaction document), likumi.lv, checked on 24 November 2026.

What people ask in Alejas before a call-out

How should I write the address so the plumber comes to Alejas in Ķekava parish and not to some other "Alejas" house in Latvia?

There are many farmsteads called “Alejas” in Latvia, so write the full address in the form: street and house number, Alejas, Ķekava parish, Ķekava Municipality. If the house has only a name and no street, add the nearest street, for example Akmeņkaļu or Priežu Street.

In Alejas the drains do not run away. How can I tell whether the septic tank is full or a pipe is blocked?

The first indicator is the receipt for the last emptying: if more time has passed since that date than usual, the septic tank is checked first. If the septic tank was emptied recently but water stands in all the fixtures, the fault is most likely in the pipe between the house and the septic tank; if in only one, then in its outlet. Clearing a drain with a cable costs from €40, clearing a blockage in the outdoor section from €50.

A 1960s house in Alejas has no sewer plan: how do I find out where the outlet runs?

Start with what is visible: inspection points, manholes in the yard and the place where the pipe leaves the foundations. If that is not enough, a video check of the drain helps, showing the direction of the pipe and the damaged spot; its price is set by the length of the pipe and access. What is found is worth drawing and keeping with the house papers.
