In the 2019 village assessment, the Olekte’s 10% flood probability risk line marks a part of Rāmava where building is restricted. For plumbing, the river and the lower spots raise one question: how to protect fixtures placed in a basement when the water level in the sewer rises.

The Olekte, the floodplain and the risk line

Rāmava is a village in the north of Ķekava parish on the banks of the Olekte, by the A7 and V2 roads. According to the village assessment, the river divides the village into two parts, detached and low-rise houses dominate the buildings, and the Olekte floodplain has large areas of farmland.

Present-day Rāmava took shape in the post-war years, but it was built up intensively at the end of the 20th century and the beginning of the 21st, when Mākoņkalns appeared on the other bank of the Olekte. Before building a basement or fitting a basement WC in a new house, it is worth knowing how close the plot is to the risk area and how high the nearest sewer manhole cover is. How everything below ground level is connected depends on that. In an older house that already has a basement, the same question comes up when you want to put a shower, a washing machine or a sauna with a drain in the basement.

Basement fixtures below the manhole cover level

Ķekava Municipality binding regulations No. 21/2022 are specific about this. Fixtures located lower than the cover of the nearest sewer manhole (for example, in a basement) must be connected to a separate sewer outlet with its own exit and an electric gate valve controlled by a sensor, or with a special non-return valve. Fixtures on the upper floors may be connected only behind this valve (points 24 and 38.4). If the system has no shut-off fittings, inspection point and non-return valve, the customer is responsible for the consequences (point 56).

If, in a house by the Olekte, the basement smells from the floor drain or water rises in the sink after heavy rain, two things need checking: whether the basement outlet runs separately and whether there is a valve on it. If not, the basement fixtures are separated onto their own outlet with a non-return valve against backflow on it, and the pipes from the upper floors are connected behind it. A basement WC or shower that sits lower than the sewer pipe needs a sewage lifting pump, and if it has stopped, sewage pump repair helps.

Groundwater in a basement is a different matter from backflow. This water is collected in a drainage pit with a pump, and it must not be discharged into the household sewer (point 49 of the regulations forbids drainage water). If the pump does not start or does not pump, there is drainage pump repair. For this work there is no single starting price: for a valve the price depends on the pipe diameter and the location, for pumps on the type of fault and access to the pit. A visit on site and assessing the fault costs from €35.

The same regulations oblige the customer to maintain their own networks up to the point set in the boundary act, to protect the pipes and the meter from flooding and freezing, and to fit automatic shut-off on basement fixtures (point 38). For a house near the Olekte this means a practical list: the meter assembly must be where it cannot be flooded, the basement fixtures need their own valve, and the basement pump must be accessible for service, not walled in behind the finish.

Basement fixtures, drainage water and responsibility: Ķekava Municipality binding regulations No. 21/2022, points 24, 38, 38.4, 49 and 56, text at likumi.lv/ta/id/338066, viewed on 23 November 2026.

Which Rāmava streets are in the third-phase network

In the project “Development of water management services in Ķekava, phase 3” (2012–2015), along Rāmavas, Dārza, Kalna, Krasta, Kāpu, Rītupes, Paegļu and Zāļu iela in Rāmava, 3,882 m of water main with hydrants and 3,419 m of sewer with 63 connection points for private houses were built. A further 456 m of pressure main and two pumping stations were built too. In these streets the project built 63 connection points at the boundaries of private houses; whether there is one at your plot can be seen on site or in the Ķekavas nami documents.

About other streets, including Mākoņkalns, this project says nothing. So in the request we ask what the house has: a connection to the street sewer or its own septic tank and its own well. The work differs: with the network, a non-return valve and a separate outlet; with its own system, the basement outlet can go to the septic tank, and then the valve position is chosen by the height of the septic tank inlet.

In the part of the village that formed after detailed plans, the streets are owned by private persons, and not everywhere were they built as planned. The population has grown from 472 (2000) to 1,380 (2021), and for new houses on private streets the connection route is set both by the technical conditions and by the street owners.

Rāmava questions about water and basements

In a Rāmava house by the Olekte the meter assembly is in the basement. What do the regulations require to keep it from flooding?

Point 38 of Ķekava Municipality binding regulations No. 21/2022 obliges the customer to protect their pipes and the meter from flooding and freezing. The general terms of the contract require the assembly to be protected from water and the temperature in it not to fall below +5 °C. If water appears in the basement repeatedly, the assembly may have to be raised or moved, and this is agreed with Ķekavas nami.

In a basement by the Olekte there is a smell from the floor drain after heavy rain. Where do I look for the cause?

First where the basement drain’s outlet joins the house sewer. If the drain is lower than the nearest manhole cover and shares an outlet with the upper floors, a full sewer can push air and water back to the lowest spot. Regulations No. 21/2022 require such fixtures to have a separate outlet with a gate valve or a special non-return valve.

In Rāmava there is a free tap by the sports ground. Is it the same network water as in my house?

If the house is connected to the Ķekavas nami water main, yes: the tap by the sports ground is a Ķekavas nami free-access drinking water point, and in summer 2026 16 m³ were drawn from it, the highest of the municipality’s three taps. If the house has its own well or borehole, the tap in the house has different water.
