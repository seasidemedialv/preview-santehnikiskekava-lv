An underfloor heating manifold without a drip tray: why the leak is noticed late

If the manifold with its valves and pump stands in a wall recess right on the floor and a joint starts to drip, the water goes under the floor covering or into the insulation. The first sign may then be swollen laminate in the next room, not a wet cabinet.

In Sweden this risk is handled by industry rules. Säker Vatten 2026:1 (4.5.1.-4.5.2.) says that a heating manifold with valves, actuators or a pump goes in a cabinet with a watertight base, raised at least 50 mm at the side walls. A leak must come out into a room where people stay and notice it easily. A room with concealed joints (4.1.5.) must have leak indication: a pipe with a fall along its whole length, an inside diameter of at least 20 mm and an outlet no closer than 60 mm to the floor waterproofing.

In an existing house a similar effect comes from a tray under the manifold and a cabinet whose doors open without tools: a drip can be seen before it reaches the floor.

Säker Vatten is a Swedish industry standard, not a Latvian requirement; it is mentioned here as an example of how to make a hidden leak visible. Checked on 24 November 2026.

A sensor on the floor or a device on the supply pipe

A leak device does not replace the repair, but it shortens the time between the first drip and the moment someone notices it. Manufacturers' data on two different principles:

LeakBot attaches to the supply pipe near where it enters the house; measures the temperature of the pipe and of the air after a prolonged inflow of colder water it detects leaks from 5 ml per minute, also in walls and under floors

A slow drip and the home policy

A small drip under a sink or at the manifold is not only a repair matter. For example, the BALTA extended risks rules (No. 1202.09) do not compensate losses caused by leaks in pipe systems, worn-out seals, or corrosion of pipes and joints. The ERGO rules (point 8.9) do not cover renewing water, sewer and heating pipes if the damage was caused by wear or rust. A slow drip is worth fixing as soon as it is noticed.

It is also useful to know the age of the pipe. For example, the If rules (No. TPI-S-20243-LV) cover repair of the leaking service itself in full if it is no older than 5 years, and 50% if up to 10 years. For an older part the repair of the part itself is not covered, but the consequences may be. Invoices or records of the pipework installation help to prove the age. The rules of every policy differ, so check your own.

A supply line under the yard: wet ground in dry weather

If the ground above the supply line route is wet even in dry weather, water may be escaping from the underground pipe between the meter chamber and the house. In the yard the leak is searched for section by section: the house is disconnected from the supply line and the pipe is checked to see whether the pressure holds.

A supply line is easier to replace later if it was laid in a sleeve. For example, Säker Vatten 2026:1 (4.1.6.) requires the water supply of a private house to be replaceable, for example in a protective sleeve that is sealed against groundwater and reaches beyond a 45° slope from the foundations. A new pipe can then be pulled through without digging under the foundations.

A visible spot and a hidden spot: two different price lines

A visible and accessible fault has the line Dripping joint or cracked pipe: locating and sealing, from €35 per spot. It covers tracing where the water escapes and changing the joint, the seal or a short length of pipe; opening up the wall or floor is not part of it.

Finding a hidden leak in the floor, wall or yard supply line has no single price. It depends on how many places the floor or wall has to be opened, whether the leak is in the house or in the yard supply line, and how many sections need a pressure test. If water is running now, start with the line Emergency call-out: shutting off the water and isolating the fault (from €35).

About hidden leaks

The floor is warm in one spot even when the heating is off. What can it mean?

A warm patch on the floor with the heating off can mean that a hot water pipe is leaking under it. Another sign is a water heater that heats more often than usual although hot water use has not changed.

Can a leak device be added to an existing supply line without opening a wall?

A device that measures the pipe temperature, such as LeakBot, is, according to the manufacturer, attached to the supply pipe near where it enters the house. For a system that shuts off the water itself, a solenoid valve has to be fitted into the pipe, so the supply is shut off for a while and the pipe is cut.

Why is a whole pipe section sometimes replaced, not just the leaking joint?

If the pipe around the joint is rusted or thin, a new joint on it will not last long. The section is then replaced up to sound pipe on both sides.
