The policy decides when repairs may start

What to do in the first days after a flood is set by the policy as well, not only by the plumbing. The rules of the policy companies differ, so here are examples from two of them, not a general law:

Rules What they require Deadline
If home rules No. TPI-S-20243-LV photograph the scene and the damage; do not start repairs without the company’s consent report within 3 working days at the latest
ERGO Home Premium cover rules No. IP 01.12.2025 inform the building management or manager and ask for a report on the cause of the incident; keep estimates and invoices if the property has not been inspected within 5 working days of the claim, repairs may start once the damage has been photographed

If the water came from a neighbour’s flat, it is worth keeping the damaged part, for example a burst hose or a rusted joint, after the repair until the report is signed. Restoring the ceiling, wall and floor finishes is a different job, and its timing is set by the terms of your or your neighbour’s policy.

The policy's helpline plumber and the building's riser

Some policies include a helpline plumber. For example, BALTA home rules (No. 1202.09) say that this plumber stops the leak or fits a temporary fix, but does not thaw pipes, drain water heaters or repair faults in shared services and risers. In the BALTA rules this service is available 3 times per policy year.

So if the water comes from a riser rather than from a neighbour’s tap or hose, the repair after the temporary shut-off usually goes through the building manager. A riser is shut off for every flat on it, so the time of the work is agreed at building level. It is worth reading your own policy before you call someone from its helpline.

The upstairs flat's valve: when it is enough

If the water comes from the upper flat’s pipes beyond its inlet, for example from a burst washing machine hose or a tap supply, closing that flat’s valve is enough. The rest of the stairwell then keeps its water.

In older buildings the flat’s valve may be boxed in behind the WC cistern or rusted so that it cannot be turned. The water is then stopped at the riser, which affects every flat on that riser. After the emergency the valve is worth replacing or making accessible, so that next time one flat is enough.

The pipes above you: when to have them checked

One flood does not yet mean the whole building’s system must be replaced, but it is a reason to look at how old the pipes are. For example, the Finnish Real Estate Federation (Kiinteistöliitto), as quoted in a press release from Finanssiala, the Finnish financial sector association, recommends a first condition survey of the pipework at 30–35 years of age and repeating it every 5–10 years after that. The same release says that detected leaks are the most common reason why pipe renovation is started there.

This is not a Latvian standard, but it is a good reference point for a residents' meeting. If a riser leaks repeatedly, the decision on riser replacement is a matter for the whole building, and the work is usually ordered by the building manager.

Questions after a flood

The policy company is not coming to inspect the damage. How long must I wait before repairing?

It depends on the policy. For example, the ERGO Premium rules allow repairs to start if the property has not been inspected within 5 working days of the claim, provided the damage has been photographed first and the estimates and invoices are kept. Other policies may set a different deadline.

The upstairs neighbour replaced the burst hose. Should the old one be thrown away?

Better not at once. A burst hose or a rusted joint is physical evidence of the cause, which can be shown to the manager who draws up the report and to the policy company. Keep it until the report is signed.

How many times a year can the policy's helpline plumber be called?

It is set by each policy. For example, under the BALTA home rules (No. 1202.09) the helpline plumber is available 3 times per policy year, and does not repair faults in risers and shared services.
