Replacing water pipes in a house - pipes older than the network connection

Replacing pipes in a house in Katlakalns, Ziedonis or Rāmava can come some time after the connection to the street water main: the supply line is new, but the pipes inside the house may be much older.

The decision whether to replace everything or only the damaged stretch starts with where the pipe rusts and where the brown water comes from. Running a new pipe in place of the old one costs from €10/m (labour), a supply or waste run to one fixture from €35.

Street water main in 2010–2015, pipes in the house older

In Katlakalns, phase II of the Cohesion Fund project (2010–2014) built 7,091 m of water main with 314 connection points. In Ziedonis, stage 3 (2012–2015) laid 8,404 m of water main, and in Rāmava the same stage laid 3,882 m along Rāmavas, Dārza, Kalna, Krasta and four other streets.

A house that connected to this network received a new supply line, but the internal pipes could stay the same as before. So one house can have three different years: the network, the supply line and the pipes in the walls. When to replace the internal pipes is decided by their condition, not by the age of the supply line. If a kitchen or bathroom in the house was already renewed after the connection, some of the pipes may be new, while stretches in the basement and between rooms remain from earlier years.

Brown water from the tap: the network or a pipe in the house

In the sample taken by Ķekavas nami on 14 May 2026 at Liliju iela 2 in Ziedonis, iron was below 0.04 mg/L, and so it was in the May analyses for Rāmava. If water from a tap turns brownish only after a water shut-off, the cause can be sediment in the network pipes, which Ķekavas nami writes about (more on the page plumber by the hour).

According to the Swedish building authority Boverket, pipe corrosion is affected by the quality of the drinking water, the age of the pipes and the quality of the material. If iron in the network analyses is below 0.04 mg/L but brown water comes from only one tap, it is worth checking the house’s own pipes on that branch.

Rust from the outside: a cold pipe in a damp basement

Boverket names a second route as well: moisture and condensation on the outside of pipes. A cold water pipe in a damp basement or under a floor can rust from the outside even though the water in it is good. Boverket writes that corrosion can end in water damage and early replacement of installations.

In such a house it is worth looking at where the pipe runs before replacing it: if the new pipe would go in the same damp place, it can be laid on another route or insulated so that condensation does not collect on it. If some metal pipes are left in the house, Annex 8 of LBN 221-15 says that copper must come after steel in the direction of water flow.

Replacing an old pipe: the work lines

Running new water pipe in place of the old one, per metre: from €10/m (labour). The price covers laying the pipe on the surface of a wall or floor and clipping it in place; pipes, fittings, chasing and restoring the finish are not in the price. Supply or waste run to one fixture: cold, hot or drain: from €35 per run. A pipe along the wall over the finish costs less than a pipe in a chase, because chasing and making good are not in the metre price.

Renewing the whole pipe system in a house has no single price. It is set by the number of fixtures and storeys, by whether the pipes run over the finish or in the wall, by the pipe material and access, and by restoring the finish after the work.

About old pipes in a house

How can I tell whether the pipe in the wall is galvanised steel or copper?

A magnet sticks to steel, but not to copper or plastic. A copper pipe is reddish brown where scratched and is usually joined with soldered fittings, while a galvanised pipe is grey with threaded sockets.

In Ziedonis the water from the network has low iron. So why do pipes in a house rust?

In the sample taken by Ķekavas nami on 14 May 2026 at Liliju iela 2 in Ziedonis, iron was below 0.04 mg/L, so there is little iron in the network water there. Boverket also names pipe age, material and moisture on the outside of pipes as causes of corrosion, so old steel can rust even with good water.
