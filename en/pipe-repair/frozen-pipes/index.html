A policy can cover frost bursts, but it may not cover a slow leak. The price for thawing a frozen supply line depends on where and over what length the pipe has frozen and on whether it has burst.

Frost burst on the policy: what is covered and when not

For example, in the BALTA extended-risk rules for homes (No. 1202.09), the liquid leakage risk includes a pipeline failure when the cause is frost. The same rules do not compensate losses from leaks in pipe systems, worn seals, or corrosion of pipes or joints. So a pipe that burst after a frost and a pipe that has been seeping slowly for years are two different cases on a policy.

What to do if a house is left unheated in winter, and how the temperature thresholds and the definition of a house that is not permanently lived in differ in the BALTA, If and ERGO rules, is set out on the page winter draining.

A running tap is not a solution: Ķekava Municipality binding regulations No. 21/2022 (point 54.6) forbid leaving taps open before the meter to prevent freezing, unless this has been agreed with the service provider.

The texts of BALTA No. 1202.09 and Ķekava Municipality binding regulations No. 21/2022 were checked on 24 November 2026.

Where a pipe freezes first: outer wall, crawl space, shallow supply line

For example, the Swedish building authority Boverket explains that water expands as it freezes, and the risk grows when a pipe sits at the outer edge of the building envelope. Pipes in an unheated crawl space need protection against frost, while pipes laid in the ground must be deep enough or insulated.

Around Ķekava this applies first of all to former summer houses: Jenči grew up as a colony of summer houses and allotments, and Jaunsils grew from a summer-house cooperative into a village of detached houses. If the water pipe in such a house was first laid for summer use, it may run shallow or along the floor above an unheated underfloor space. How to convert such a house for winter is described on the page garden house all year round.

A garden tap that bursts with a hose attached

An outdoor tap is the end of a pipe that sits in the cold. The manufacturer FM Mattsson (Mora frost-proof tap 4296) says to fit it through the wall with the valve body in a heated room, drilling a Ø20-25 mm hole in the wall that slopes 2–3° outwards. When the tap is closed the pipe empties itself, but before winter the quick coupling and hose must be removed, otherwise the water cannot run out and may freeze.

The Swedish Säker Vatten rules ask for more: an outdoor tap must have drainage that empties it when the tap is closed, even if a hose is attached. This is not a requirement in Latvia, but it is worth knowing when you choose a new tap.

Thawing and the outdoor tap: what the price depends on

The line Thawing a frozen supply line or pipe has no single price. It depends on where the pipe has frozen (in the house, in the basement or in the ground), how long the frozen stretch is, and whether the pipe has burst.

The price of a garden tap on the outside wall with an indoor winter shut-off is also set by the situation: by the wall material and thickness, the length of the pipe run to the tap, and whether a frost-proof tap is needed. If a crack is found after thawing, it has the line Dripping joint or cracked pipe: locating and sealing, from €35 per spot.

About frost and pipes

A sewer pipe froze in the yard. Can a cable be put inside it?

No. The manufacturer Nexans states that for sewer pipes a heating cable may only be laid on the outside of the pipe. For a water pipe the same manufacturer allows a DEFROST WATER cable also inside the pipe, up to 60 m.

Should the valve indoors also be closed on a frost-proof tap in winter?

A frost-proof tap empties itself when the hose is removed, but a valve before the tap gives extra safety. Close it and open the outdoor tap so that the remaining water runs out.
