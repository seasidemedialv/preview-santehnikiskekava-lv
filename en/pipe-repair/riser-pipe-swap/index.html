A shut-off valve that must be reachable even when nobody is in the flat

LBN 221-15 (point 120) states that riser shut-off fittings must be accessible at any time, and must not be placed in rooms whose users may be away, for example a locked flat.

When risers are replaced, the valves are therefore put in the basement or another shared space. An old building can be the other way round: the riser valve sits behind the pipe boxing in a flat’s toilet, and in an emergency the water can be shut off only if that neighbour is at home.

LBN 221-15 sets design requirements for new and rebuilt water supply systems. Checked on 24 November 2026 on likumi.lv.

A valve for each flat: the Swedish requirement as a model

For example, the Swedish industry rules Säker Vatten 2026:1 (4.1.7) require that water can be shut off separately for each flat or room and that the shut-off valves are easy to reach. This is not a Latvian standard, but it is a practical idea for riser replacement: if every flat has its own working valve on its supply, the next leak in one flat does not require shutting off the whole riser.

So a flat’s valve belongs right after the branch from the riser, where it can be turned by hand, not behind the wall of a tiled box. If the old valve is walled in, replacing the riser is the moment to move it.

An Altum loan and the owners' decision for the whole building

A loan can help with larger work. Under Cabinet Regulation No. 481 (6 July 2021), in the programme for blocks of flats where loans are issued by Altum, the supported work includes renewing, rebuilding or creating all kinds of the building’s engineering systems. The maximum loan term is 20 years, and an application needs the flat owners' decision on the building work; whether a particular building qualifies is assessed by Altum.

This also applies to the older buildings around Ķekava: for example, in Titurga about 500 flats were built by the end of the 1980s. The decision on replacing all the risers and on a loan is taken at building level, and replacing a one-flat stretch is agreed with the building manager.

The price of a riser stretch in one flat

Water riser replacement in a flat, one storey high, costs from €110 per storey. It covers a stretch of cold or hot water riser in one flat with the transitions to the neighbouring stretches.

The line “Ø110 soil stack in a flat: cast-iron section replaced with plastic” costs from €100 per storey. On both lines, agreeing the work with the building manager and materials are extra.

The price changes with the amount of work, access (box, tiles or shaft) and materials. The new stretch joins the neighbouring stretches above and below, so it matters what they are made of and how easy they are to reach. If risers are replaced for the whole building, the work is ordered by the building manager or the owners together; more on this on the page for building managers.

About risers and the building's decision

In our building the riser valve is in one neighbour's toilet, and he is often away. Can it stay like that?

LBN 221-15 (point 120) is a design requirement for new and rebuilt systems: in them, riser shut-off fittings must not be placed in rooms whose users may be away. If the building plans to replace risers, moving the valves to the basement or another shared space is worth including in the description of the work.

Can an Altum loan be used only for replacing risers?

Cabinet Regulation No. 481 lists renewing, rebuilding or creating a block of flats' engineering systems among the supported work. Whether a particular project qualifies is assessed by Altum, and the application needs the flat owners' decision.

Can the water riser and the soil stack in a flat be replaced at the same time?

They can, and these are two separate price lines, each per storey. If both risers run in one box, doing the work together means the box has to be opened only once. Both need the building manager’s agreement, because both risers serve the whole building.

Are the Swedish Säker Vatten requirements mandatory in Latvia?

No. They are Swedish industry rules and are mentioned here as a model. In Latvia, the design requirements for water supply systems are set by LBN 221-15, for example point 120 on access to riser valves.
