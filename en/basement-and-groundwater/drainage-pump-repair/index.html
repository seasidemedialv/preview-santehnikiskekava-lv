Drainage pump repair - a pit that dries out in summer

Repairing a drainage pump starts with what happened in the pit: whether there was enough water in it, whether the pump pulled in mud and whether the pit dried out in summer.

Manufacturers' manuals give exact figures for these cases, for example for Calpeda GXR and GXV pumps, and they help show whether the pump is faulty or only placed wrongly. There is no single price for checking and repairing.

Air around the rotor after a dry spell

If a basement pit has water only in spring and autumn, it can dry out in summer. When the water returns, an air pocket can remain around the pump’s rotor: the motor turns, but the water does not move. For this case the Calpeda GXR/GXV manual gives a remedy: if the pit can dry out, a 3 mm hole is drilled in the discharge pipe before the non-return valve, so that the air around the rotor can escape.

Such a hole is inside the pit, not outside, and while the pump runs a little water squirts back into the pit through it. If your pump hums after the summer but does not pump, check this first and only then the motor.

How much water the pump needs in the pit

For its GXR and GXV submersible pumps the manufacturer Calpeda states the minimum water level both for operation with a float switch and for manual mode:

Pump With a float switch In manual mode
Calpeda GXR 70 mm 15 mm
Calpeda GXV 130 mm 30 mm

If the pit is shallow or the pump stands on a raised base, the float can switch the pump off before the water is pumped out, or not switch it on at all. The same manual allows no more than 30 starts an hour at even intervals, so a pump that keeps switching on and off has to be looked at together with the pit and the float setting.

In winter and after muddy water

The Calpeda GXR/GXV manual has two instructions that matter more to a basement with seasonal water than the motor data. If there is a risk of frost, the pump is taken out of the water and stored in a dry place. If the pump has pumped muddy water, it is then run briefly in clean water so that the silt does not dry inside.

In an unheated basement or a pit outside the house this means autumn work: the pump is lifted out, rinsed and put somewhere warm, and in spring it is put back before the first water and the float is checked. If the pump hums in spring but does not pump, the fault may not be in the motor but in silt that dried over winter or in a stuck float.

Power supply and discharge before the repair

If the pump is completely silent, first see whether the residual-current device or a fuse has tripped, but do not touch anything electrical while standing in the pit or in water. The pump is checked outside the pit, disconnected from the mains.

If the discharge pipe is also changed during the repair, the water is taken out of the house, not into the sewer: in the “Ķekavas nami” network it is forbidden to send drainage water into the centralised sewer. Where it may be sent is covered in the guide on groundwater in a basement.

The faulty part and access to the pit set the price

The price of checking and repairing depends on the faulty part (float, valve or motor), on access to the pit and on whether repair still pays off against a new pump. If it is cheaper to replace the pump and the pit or the discharge also has to be reworked, the price is set by the pit, the length of the discharge and the electrical connection.

About repairing a drainage pump

Does the hole drilled in the discharge pipe harm how the pump works?

No, if it is as the manufacturer states. Calpeda specifies 3 mm, before the non-return valve and inside the pit, to let air out. Only a small jet of water returns to the pit through it.

Can the pump be run by hand if the float is stuck?

Briefly and under supervision. In manual mode Calpeda gives a minimum water level of 15 mm (GXR) and 30 mm (GXV), and below that the pump starts to draw air. A float that is stuck in silt or caught behind the pit wall has to be freed or replaced.

In spring the pump pumped muddy water for several days. Does it have to be replaced now?

Not necessarily. After muddy water Calpeda advises running the pump briefly in clean water so that the silt does not dry inside. If it still does not pump or overheats after that, the pump is lifted out and checked, and then it is decided whether repair still pays off.
