Sewage pump repair - the lifting pump behind a basement toilet

Sewage pump repair on this page concerns a sewage lifting pump inside the house: the unit behind a basement toilet, shower or sink that lifts the waste up to the soil stack.

Yard sewage pumping stations are a different job and are not covered here. There is no single price for checking, repairing or replacing: it is set by the kind of fault, the spare parts and whether the pump has to be lifted out of its pit.

Smell in the basement and the class of the unit

The manufacturer Jung Pumpen, referring to EN 12050, divides these units by what they pump. Units for waste containing faecal matter (EN 12050-1) have to be vented above the roof, and venting valves are not permitted for them. Units for waste without faecal matter (EN 12050-2) and limited-use units (EN 12050-3) may be vented in the room where they stand, with an activated-carbon filter reducing the smell.

If the basement smells of sewage, first find out which class your unit belongs to. In a small unit behind a toilet (limited use, EN 12050-3) the carbon filter may be worn out, and it is replaced. In a larger faecal unit without venting up to the roof the smell is a question of the connection, not of the filter, and the repair is then building a vent pipe.

The discharge pipe: first up, then sideways

In the SFA Saniflo manual (Sanivite) the discharge pipe has three rules:

  • The vertical rise has to come before the horizontal run.
  • After the horizontal run the pipe must not go upward again.
  • In the calculation 3 feet (about 0.9 m) vertical equals 30 feet (about 9 m) horizontal, and each 90° bend reduces the rise by about 3 feet.

If after a basement rebuild the unit pumps slowly or non-stop, the cause may be a discharge pipe that has been rerouted with extra bends or a second climb. Then the repair is rerouting the pipe, not replacing the pump.

Hot water and a greywater unit

Not every basement unit is meant for a toilet. For example, according to SFA Saniflo the greywater unit Sanivite P110 pumps water up to 60 °C and Sanishower up to 40 °C, and after prolonged hotter water the thermal protection trips: the unit cools for about an hour and switches on by itself. So a unit that goes quiet after a hot laundry programme and later works again may not be faulty. Saniflo allows a washing machine to be connected only indirectly, through a sink.

Gate valve, non-return valve and the alarm

Jung Pumpen states that for sewage lifting units (EN 12050-1) a shut-off valve in the discharge pipe is mandatory so that the unit can be disconnected for servicing, and a non-return valve is mandatory too. The control unit goes in a dry, well-ventilated place protected from flooding, with an alarm signal for each connected dwelling.

If the discharge pipe has no shut-off valve, the unit cannot be disconnected for repair without letting out everything that stands in the pipe. In such a case it is worth fitting a shut-off valve together with the repair.

While the unit is not working, do not use anything connected to it: the basement toilet, shower, sink and, if it is connected, the washing machine. Fixtures on the upper floors that drain to the stack by gravity can be used. If the unit has an alarm and it sounds, the water in the tank is already high.

Motor, cutter or valve: what the price depends on

The price of checking, repairing or replacing a lifting pump is set by the kind of fault (motor, cutter, valve), the availability of spare parts and whether the pump has to be lifted out of the pit. If a new unit has to go in place of the old one, the price is set by the number of fixtures to connect, the length of the discharge pipe to the stack and the electrical connection.

About repairing a lifting pump

There is another toilet upstairs in the house. Does the basement unit need an alarm?

For sewage lifting units (EN 12050-1) Jung Pumpen requires an alarm for each connected dwelling. For a small unit behind a WC it is set by the manufacturer’s manual, but an alarm is a cheap way to learn of a stoppage before water appears on the floor.

Why is a carbon filter enough for one unit and another needs venting up to the roof?

It depends on the class. According to Jung Pumpen, units for faecal matter (EN 12050-1) are vented above the roof, while units for waste without faecal matter and limited-use units may be vented in the room through a carbon filter.

The unit no longer has spare parts. What then?

Then it is replaced with a new one. A new unit may have different connections and discharge-pipe requirements, so the price is set by the number of fixtures to connect, the length of the discharge pipe and the electrical connection.
