Groundwater in a basement in spring - what you can do before a pump

Groundwater in a basement in spring means that the groundwater level around the house has risen above the basement floor or the wall joints, and the water comes in through the foundations, not through the sewer.

Before buying a pump, it is worth finding out how high and for how long the water stands, because that decides what kind of pit and pump are needed. The groundwater level can change by more than a metre over a year (LVĢMC data for 2023).

Note down the level before you buy a pump

The simplest measurement is a mark on the basement wall and a date. Write down when the water appears, how high it rises and for how many days it stays. If the basement has a pit or a hole in the floor, the water level in it can be measured with a ruler every few days.

These notes answer two questions. First, whether the water comes every spring or only in some years, because then it is a matter of a permanent solution, not a one-off pumping out. Second, how much water has to be removed, because the size of the pit and the capacity of the pump should be chosen by the inflow, not by the floor area of the basement.

Why groundwater changes around Ķekava

The LVĢMC 2023 report states that the groundwater level regime is also affected by intensive water extraction around Riga, by reservoirs, for example the Riga hydropower plant reservoir, and by land drainage systems. These changes usually cover small, local areas, and even in neighbouring boreholes the levels can differ considerably. So the neighbour’s basement is not a measure for yours.

Annual swings
in 2023 the amplitude of the summer fall at the observation stations was 0.35–1.67 m, and the amplitude of the autumn and winter rise was 0.35–1.63 m.
Level against earlier years
in 2023 it was 0.04–0.28 m higher than in 2022 (except Lielupe and Remte) and at most stations 0.07–0.55 m higher than the long-term average.
Daugmale
the detailed plan “Daugavparks” states that the long-term average level of the Riga reservoir at this cross-section is 17.81 m LAS, and with a 10% probability (once in 10 years) 18.25 m LAS. This applies to this place, not to the whole municipality.

Ditches and drainage around the house

Not everything is decided by the weather. In an e-consultation on the LV portal in 2023, a resident of Ķekava Municipality wrote that after a land drainage ditch and wells had been filled in, the water level in the basements of houses had risen by as much as 0.7 m. The municipality replied that the owners of a land drainage system are responsible for its operation.

If water appeared in the basement after something changed in the surroundings, for example a ditch filled in, a yard paved or a new building put up next door, start with where the water used to run off. A pump will remove the water, but where a ditch has been filled in, it will work every spring.

Where the water may be pumped: Ķekava rules

If a house is connected to the Ķekavas nami sewer, groundwater and drainage water must not be let into it. When connecting the house, the owner even confirms this in writing. The pump’s discharge is therefore taken to the ground surface, a ditch or the rainwater system, if there is one, not into the sewer inspection chamber.

Ķekava Municipality binding regulations No. 21/2022, points 14.4, 49.8 and 49.9; text compared on likumi.lv on 24 November 2026. For example, the home policy rules of If, No. TPI-S-20243-LV do not count groundwater getting into a basement as a flood and do not compensate losses from groundwater that enters a building regularly, for example every spring. The rules of your policy may differ.

The price of the pump and pit work

Setting a drainage pump with a float switch in a pit, with a discharge line, has no single price. It is set by whether the pit already exists or has to be made, how long the discharge line is and where the water is sent, and how power is brought to the pump. If a pump is already there but does not start or does not pump, the price of checking and repair depends on whether the fault is in the float, the valve or the motor and whether repair pays off against replacement.

Steps from the first water to a pump

  1. Note it down. A mark on the wall, the date, the height and the number of days each time water appears.
  2. Check the surroundings. Whether a ditch, culvert or drain outlet around the house has been filled in or overgrown, and who owns it.
  3. Choose the spot. Put the pump pit where the basement floor is lowest, since water gathers there first.
  4. Discharge outside. The pressure line is taken to the ground surface or a ditch away from the house, if the ditch is yours or its owner agrees, not into the sewer.

Questions: Groundwater in a basement in spring

May groundwater be pumped into a sewer chamber if the house is on the Ķekavas nami network?

No. Ķekava Municipality binding regulations No. 21/2022 forbid discharging drainage water and groundwater into the centralised sewer, and when connecting the house the owner confirms this in writing. The discharge is taken to the ground surface, a ditch or the rainwater system.

A neighbour filled in the ditch, and since then there has been water in my basement. Where do I turn?

In an e-consultation on the LV portal, Ķekava Municipality replied that the owners of a land drainage system are responsible for its operation. Start with who owns the ditch or the drainage, and note down when the water appeared.

A house in Daugmale is by the reservoir. Will the water in the basement follow its level?

Not necessarily directly. The difference in the detailed plan “Daugavparks” between the average reservoir level and the level possible once in 10 years is 0.44 m, while LVĢMC states that groundwater levels can differ considerably even in neighbouring boreholes. The notes from your own basement will tell you more than the reservoir’s figures.
