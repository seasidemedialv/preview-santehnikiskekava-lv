A connection with municipal co-financing

In Cohesion Fund projects from 2010 to 2015, networks were built in Katlakalns, Odukalns, Vimbukrogs, Ziedonis and Rāmava along some of the streets (eight in Rāmava) with connection points at plot boundaries, but a house there may still have its own septic tank. Ķekava Municipality binding regulations No. 14/2023 with amendments in force from 6 March 2026 let the municipality cover the network section up to €2,500 per detached house; an application has 60 days after the notice is published. Who can apply and what stays with you: see co-financing for a connection. Co-financing does not cover the house’s internal networks; the owner builds the service pipe from the connection point to the house under the technical conditions of SIA “Ķekavas nami”.

Water in the basement every spring

If water appears on the basement floor in spring and comes in through the foundations, it is groundwater, not sewage. Near the Daugava its level is also affected by the reservoir: the Daugmale detailed plan “Daugavparks” (2024) says the average Riga reservoir level there is 17.81 m LAS, and once in 10 years it can be 18.25 m LAS. How to note down the level in your basement and where water may be pumped before you buy a pump: see groundwater in a basement. In the LVĢMC 2023 report the level at observation stations changed by over a metre in a year and can differ greatly even between neighbouring boreholes. Water coming through a floor drain or a toilet is a different case: see water in the basement.

A sink or shower drains slowly

In a house with its own septic tank a blockage is not only a pipe question, because everything you pour down the sink ends up in your yard tank. That is why the article water will not drain also covers what a septic tank will not tolerate and why a chemical cleaner harms it. For houses on the Ķekavas nami network, regulations No. 21/2022 (in force from 17 December 2022) forbid in point 49 discharging into the sewer grease, solids and floating insoluble substances larger than 4 mm. Latvijas Zaļais punkts advises putting wet wipes in household waste, including those marked as flushable (jauns.lv, 4 June 2026).

A radiator heats more weakly than the others

In the former garden societies, some summer houses have become detached houses, for example in Mellupi, as noted by the 2019 village assessment, and the heating circuit of such a house may contain antifreeze left from when the house stood unheated in winter. The manufacturer KORAD states that a 35% antifreeze reduces the heat output of radiators by 10%, while a radiator connected the wrong way round loses 30–40%. The article radiator not heating also covers a reversed connection, curtains and pump pressure in a house where the owner looks after the circuit.

A bath or a shower in the new bathroom

In a house connected to the network, every litre is paid for twice: for water and for sewerage. The SIA “Ķekavas nami” tariff in the villages of Ķekava parish and in Daugmale from 1 January 2026 is €1.09/m³ for water and €3.04/m³ for sewerage, excluding VAT, and in Baloži €0.59 and €1.60/m³ (SPRK decision No. 110, 27 November 2025). The article bath or shower works out what this means for one bath and one shower, how to measure the flow of your own shower and why the water heater and the position of the drain also decide it.

About the advice articles

The articles mention Swedish, Finnish and US recommendations. Are they mandatory in Latvia?

No. They are examples that show well why something is done a certain way. In Latvia and Ķekava Municipality the mandatory rules are named in the articles with a number and a point, for example Ķekava Municipality binding regulations No. 21/2022 or the building code LBN 231-15.

Does the co-financing article also suit Iecava or Salaspils?

Only in part. Ķekava Municipality binding regulations No. 14/2023 apply to Ķekava Municipality, including Baloži and Baldone. In Iecava, Bauska Municipality binding regulations No. 4 are in force, which provide co-financing only for a sewer connection, up to €1,500. For other municipalities, ask your water management company.
