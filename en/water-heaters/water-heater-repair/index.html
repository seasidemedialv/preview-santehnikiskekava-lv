Repairing a water heater that won’t heat or leaks: element, thermostat, valve costs from €45 per job around Ķekava, spare parts extra. The thermometer on the casing, the reset button and the safety valve each say something different, and which part is replaced depends on that.

The casing thermometer is not a diagnosis

If a water heater has a thermometer on its casing, it is easy to take its reading for the water temperature. The manufacturer Gorenje (TGR N series) states that the casing thermometer shows only an approximate temperature at the place of installation, while the thermostat regulates the water in the lower part of the tank, so the two readings can differ.

A low thermometer reading after filling a bath does not yet mean a fault: cold water has flowed into the tank, and the thermostat has only just switched heating on. Something else points to a fault: the tap gives lukewarm water for a long time although the water heater is switched on and there has been no heavy use.

In the same Gorenje diagram the thermostat has a two-pole thermal fuse. It cuts the power if the water overheats, so its tripping usually points to a thermostat that did not switch the heating off in time.

The reset button and when it no longer helps

In the fault section for its Super S series of stainless steel water heaters, the manufacturer OSO gives a sequence: if there is no hot water, check the power supply, press the red reset button and turn the thermostat down by about 5°. If there is still no hot water, an authorised repairer must be called. For other manufacturers' water heaters the sequence may differ, so start with your own heater’s instructions.

What matters most is what happens after the reset. If the water heater heats and the protection no longer trips, the thermostat may have been set right at its limit. If the protection trips again and again, the cause has stayed in the tank, and it is usually looked for in the thermostat or the heating element. Replacing these parts is covered by the water heater repair line.

A safety valve nobody has turned for years

Water under a water heater can come from the safety valve, and manufacturers have these valves checked regularly. For example, for the OSO safety valve SV-K, which trips at about 9 bar, an annual check is required: the valve is opened for 1 minute by turning the handle through 90°, and you make sure that water runs freely into the drain.

If no water flows, the manufacturer’s instruction is to switch off the power, close the water and contact the installer. A valve that does not open cannot release pressure when the water expands as it heats. If after the check the valve no longer closes and drips, it too has to be replaced.

The manufacturer also specifies a minimum inside diameter of 18 mm for the drain pipe. If in an old house the valve’s small pipe runs into a narrow hose or ends in the air, drops land on the floor or in the cabinet. The drain can be sorted out together with replacing the valve.

The repair line from €45 and what the water heater model decides

Repairing a water heater that won’t heat or leaks: element, thermostat, valve costs from €45 per job. It includes finding the fault and replacing the heating element, the thermostat or the safety valve; spare parts are extra.

The price changes with the amount of work (for example, whether the water heater has to be drained to replace the element), access (a water heater above a WC, in a cabinet or under the ceiling) and materials: the price of a spare part depends on the manufacturer and the model. If the water heater is replaced with a new one, that is another line: water heater installation, from €80 per heater.

A heating element and a thermostat are not universal parts: they have to match the water heater’s model, power and flange size. That is why the label on the side of the water heater, which gives the manufacturer, model, volume and power, helps to work out before the job which part is needed and whether it is still available.

If the water heater still does not heat

After a reset the water heater heats, but the next day it stops again. What does that mean?

A repeated trip of the protection means that the cause has stayed. OSO states: if after the reset there is still no hot water, an authorised repairer must be called. If the protection trips repeatedly, the cause has stayed in the tank, and it is usually looked for in the thermostat or the heating element; replacing both is covered by the water heater repair line.

The safety valve's small pipe disappears into the wall. How can I see whether water runs during the check?

The water must appear at the point where the small pipe enters the drain, for example a funnel or a trap. If the pipe disappears into the wall and the outlet cannot be seen anywhere, you cannot be sure the valve works. It is then worth moving the drain so that it is visible.

The water heater is heated from a heating boiler and also has an element. Which part does this line repair?

The water heater repair line covers replacing the heating element, the thermostat or the safety valve, that is, the electrical part of the tank and the valve. If hot water runs short while the heating boiler is running, the tank’s connection to the heating circuit has to be looked at; its price depends on the position of the tank and its distance from the heating boiler, see water heater installation.

Water is running from the bottom of the water heater and is not decreasing. Which valve stops it?

The tank is refilled by the cold water pipe entering the water heater, and the valve on it stops new water flowing in. Switch off the power as OSO requires after a failed valve check, but switch off the water heater’s circuit breaker at the fuse board only if it is dry there. If the water does not stop, the line Emergency call-out: shutting off the water and isolating the fault, from €35 per call-out, suits.
