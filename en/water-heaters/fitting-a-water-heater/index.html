Before buying, two things can already be compared in the shop: the tank’s energy label and the tank material. The first shows how much heat the tank loses, the second whether it suits the water that comes into the house from the network or from your own well.

A label that is needed even for a tank without a heating element

EU Regulation 812/2013 (Article 3) requires every hot water storage tank to have a printed energy label, including an indirect hot-water cylinder that has no heater of its own. The class on the label is set by the tank’s heat losses.

A water heater keeps the water warm even when nobody opens a tap. The heat the tank loses in a utility room or a basement has to be produced again, whether by an electric heating element, a heating boiler or a heat pump. That is why the label is useful exactly for comparing:

two tanks of the same volume
the class shows which of them keeps heat better;
a tank that will stand in an unheated room
there the heat losses do not warm the house, they just go;
replacing an old water heater
even a new tank without a heating element, connected to a heating boiler, comes with its own label.

EU Regulation 812/2013, Article 3 (suppliers' obligations on the label for storage tanks), text viewed on legislation.gov.uk on 24 November 2026.

Tank material, if the water comes from your own well

Water heaters also differ in what the tank is made of. The manufacturer OSO does not fit an anode as standard in its stainless steel tanks: it is installed only if the water contains more than 50 mg/l of chlorides, while the upper limit in the manufacturer’s terms is 250 mg/l. So for such a tank the composition of the water is a question to settle when buying.

In a house connected to the network the water quality is monitored by the water supplier, but with your own well or borehole only a water analysis shows the chlorides. If you have one, it is worth taking it along when choosing a water heater. How the anode is then looked after in an enamelled or stainless steel tank is described on the page about water heater descaling.

A coil from the heating boiler and the 80 °C limit

In an indirect hot-water cylinder the hot water is heated by heating water flowing through a coil inside the tank. Some of these cylinders also have an electric heating element that heats the water when the heating boiler is not running.

The manufacturer NIBE (BA-ST cylinders) recommends limiting the boiler flow temperature to the cylinder’s coils to 80 °C. The reason is the thermal protection of the electric heating element: the manufacturer wants it not to trip from the heating boiler’s heat.

The settings of a heating boiler or a heat pump are not part of fitting a water heater; in this job the cylinder is connected to the flow and return of the heating circuit and to the water supply. More on the heating circuit on the heating systems page.

What the line from €80 includes and what changes the sum for a tank by a heating boiler

Mounting an electric water heater and connecting it to the water pipes costs from €80 per heater. It includes fixing the water heater to a finished wall and connecting it to the cold and hot water with a safety valve. The water heater itself, the electric supply and removing the old water heater are not in the price.

Connecting an indirect hot-water cylinder to a heating boiler or heat pump has no single price. It is set by the volume and position of the cylinder, the distance to the heating boiler or heat pump, and whether a circulation loop is needed, along which hot water circulates back to the cylinder.

The price of an electric water heater changes with the amount of work (whether the new heater goes in the old place on the same pipes), access (a narrow recess, a cabinet, a spot above a washing machine) and materials. If the pipes have to be moved, each new run is charged as the line Supply or waste run to one fixture: cold, hot or drain, from €35 per run.

Before buying a water heater

Two tanks have the same volume but a different class on the label. What does that mean?

The class of a storage tank is set by its heat losses, that is, by how much heat it loses while the water stands in it. A tank with a better class loses less heat, and that heat does not have to be produced again.

The well water analysis shows 80 mg/l of chlorides. Does a stainless steel water heater suit?

For its stainless steel tanks OSO states that an anode is fitted if there are more than 50 mg/l of chlorides, and the upper limit is 250 mg/l. With 80 mg/l, according to OSO, such a tank needs an anode. For another manufacturer’s tank, see its instructions.

The new water heater is bigger than the old one. Will the pipes have to be moved?

If the new water heater’s connections are at a different height or distance, the pipes are extended or moved. Where a new pipe to the water heater is needed, it is charged as the line Supply or waste run to one fixture: cold, hot or drain (from €35 per run). The new water heater’s instruction sheet with the connection dimensions helps before the work.
