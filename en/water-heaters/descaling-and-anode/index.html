The biggest difference between water heaters is in the anode: in an enamelled tank the magnesium anode wears away gradually, in a tank fed by a heating boiler its condition can be measured, and a stainless steel tank may have no anode at all. That is why the water heater’s label with the manufacturer and the model helps before the job.

An enamelled tank: an anode that announces its own replacement

In the tank of an enamelled water heater, corrosion protection is provided by a magnesium anode. It is a part that dissolves over time, so at a service the anode is unscrewed, inspected and, if it is worn, replaced with a new one.

Some manufacturers report the anode’s condition themselves. The manufacturer Stiebel Eltron fits its wall-mounted SHZ S water heaters (30–150 l) with a SERVICE ANODE indicator: when it lights up, the anode has to be replaced by a specialist. The same instructions allow descaling only with the flange removed, and say never to treat the tank surface and the anode with descaling agents.

Other water heaters have no indicator, and the anode’s condition can then be seen only when it is unscrewed. That is why the service line includes an anode check, while a new anode is a separate material whose size depends on the tank’s manufacturer.

A tank by a heating boiler: the anode current is measured without unscrewing it

For indirect hot-water cylinders heated by a heating boiler or a heat pump, the anode can be checked by another method. The manufacturer NIBE (BA-ST double-coil cylinders) recommends checking the condition of the insulated magnesium anode once a year by measuring the protective current with a milliammeter: with a full tank it must be at least 0.3 mA.

  • If the anode has lost more than 50%, NIBE says to replace it at once.
  • Even with no visible wear, NIBE replaces the anode no later than after 18 months.

The connection of the cylinder’s coil to the heating circuit stays as it was in this service: the work is on the water side of the tank, the sediment, the anode and the safety valve. If the cylinder itself has to be connected afresh, that is described on the water heater installation page.

A stainless steel tank that needs an anode only with chlorides

The manufacturer OSO does not fit an anode as standard in its stainless steel tanks. It is installed only if the water contains more than 50 mg/l of chlorides, while the upper limit in the manufacturer’s terms is 250 mg/l.

For such a water heater, cleaning means the sediment and the safety valve, while the anode question is decided by a water analysis. If the water in the house comes from its own well or borehole, the chloride figure can only be learned in a laboratory, and it is useful before an anode is or is not put in the tank.

Sulphates in the taps of Iecava, Zorģi, Zālīte and Dzimtmisa

Bauskas novada komunālserviss (BNKS) has published LVĢMC test report No. 24A01416 on tap samples taken on 13 May 2024. The sulphates in them were: in Iecava, 21A E. Virzas iela, 59.2 mg/l, in Zorģi 58.3 mg/l, in Zālīte 120 mg/l, and in Dzimtmisa 478 mg/l. BNKS states that several villages have an elevated amount of sulphates, but that the water is not dangerous to health.

Sulphates matter for a water heater because a magnesium anode can react with them (see the question below). In the BNKS water-main samples the sulphates in Iecava, Zorģi, Zālīte and Dzimtmisa were 58.3–478 mg/l; the water of your own well may differ even more, and only an analysis shows it. For houses on the Iecava side with an enamelled water heater, this is one more reason to inspect the anode at a service rather than just clean out the sediment.

LVĢMC test report No. 24A01416 (samples of 13 May 2024) is published on bnks.lv; the BNKS explanation about water quality was viewed on 24 November 2026.

The service line and the parts that come extra

Routine water-heater service: scale, anode, safety valve costs from €75 per heater. It includes draining the water heater, removing the sediment, checking the condition of the anode and the safety valve, and refilling. A new anode is extra.

The price changes with the amount of work (how much sediment is in the tank and whether the anode unscrews), access (a water heater in a cabinet, above a WC or under the ceiling) and materials: the price of an anode depends on the tank’s manufacturer. If the check shows a faulty heating element or thermostat, that is water heater repair, from €45 per job.

About the anode and the water

The hot water smells of rotten eggs, the cold does not. Is it the anode?

It may be. Water quality materials from North Dakota State University (NDSU, USA) explain that if the smell is only in the hot water, the sulphates in the water react with the water heater’s magnesium anode and form hydrogen sulphide. As a solution NDSU names replacing the anode with an aluminium one or removing it, but advises testing the water first. Removing the anode without the tank manufacturer’s permission is not advisable. If the cold water smells too, the cause is in the water source, not in the water heater.

The anode of a NIBE cylinder looks sound. Is it replaced anyway?

For its BA-ST cylinders NIBE states that the anode is replaced no later than after 18 months even if wear is not visible. Between replacements its condition is measured with a milliammeter with the tank full.

In Dzimtmisa the water comes from the BNKS water main. What does 478 mg/l of sulphates mean for a water heater?

It was the highest figure of the four samples of 13 May 2024, several times more than in Iecava. In an enamelled water heater with a magnesium anode it means more substance for the anode to react with, so it is worth inspecting the anode at a service. For an OSO stainless steel tank, the manufacturer ties the need for an anode to chlorides, not to sulphates.
