The fall in the shower area: 7 to 20 mm per metre

For example, the Swedish wet-room industry rules GVK Säkra Våtrum 2021:1 (5.8.3) say that in the shower area the floor fall towards the drain is from 1:150 (7 mm/m) to 1:50 (20 mm/m). On the rest of a wet-room floor it is from 1:500 (2 mm/m) to 1:100 (10 mm/m), and between the drain and the nearest walls up to 1:25 (40 mm/m). These are Swedish industry figures, not a Latvian standard (viewed on 24 November 2026).

If the drain is 0.9 m from the farthest wall, with these falls the floor at the wall is 6–18 mm higher than at the drain. The top edge of the drain has to be at the lowest point, and below it there is still the drain body and the waste pipe. Together this decides whether the new floor stays at the same level as the next room, or whether there will be a threshold or a step at the door.

This fall is the slope of the floor surface towards the drain, not the fall of the sewer pipe. Each has its own requirements, and they are not mixed up.

A cabin with a tray or a floor with a drain

A tray stands on the floor, and its trap connects where the bath’s waste used to be, if the height allows; the floor stays as it is, and the job is shower cabin installation. In a walk-in shower the water runs over the tiles to the drain, and the drain needs an outlet below the floor level, so the position and height of the drain are decided before the floor is redone.

A bath tap on the wall or a new shower column

The bath mixer tap on the wall can stay if it has a shower diverter and is at the needed height. If instead of it you want a column with an overhead shower or a thermostat, that is shower system installation, and the existing outlets are checked for whether they suit the new set.

Bath, shower and a septic tank in a private house

The British organisation Waterwise estimates that a typical bath uses about 80 litres of water, a standard shower head 10–15 litres a minute, and an economical one about 6 litres a minute. A short shower therefore uses less than a bath, but a long shower with a big head can use as much.

In a house with its own well and a septic tank or holding tank around Ķekava, all this water ends up in the tank. With an economical shower head the holding tank fills more slowly, but only if you do not stand in the shower longer than you would lie in the bath.

When a bath becomes a shower

The old bath's water pipes are left in the floor. What is done with them?

In the shower spot they do not stay. The Swedish Säker Vatten 2026:1 rules (4.6.1) allow in the floor of a shower or bath spot only the drain hole, so the old runs in the floor are capped or rebuilt into the wall. Each new run is priced as “Supply or waste run to one fixture: cold, hot or drain”, from €35 per run.

How wide an area around the drain needs the steepest fall in the floor?

In its wet-room guidance the City of Vaasa building control in Finland recommends a floor fall of at least 1:100, and 1:50 within about 0.5 m of the drain. So in a half-metre strip around the drain the floor drops by about 10 mm (our calculation), and more gently further out. This is a Finnish municipal recommendation, not a Latvian standard.

The house has a septic tank. Does a shower load it less than a bath?

If the shower is short, yes. By Waterwise figures a bath is about 80 litres, while a standard shower is 10–15 litres a minute, so after 5–8 minutes the shower uses the same. With an economical head at about 6 litres a minute that is more than 13 minutes.
