A common part of this work is the silicone joint between the tray and the wall, and it has its own rules: the temperature it is applied at, and the depth of the joint against its width. If the cabin is in a garden house that stands unheated in winter, renewing the joint needs a warm room.

Silicone in a cold bathroom

The joint between the tray and the wall, and between the glass profiles and the tray, is made of sanitary silicone. When it peels away or turns black, it is cut out and a new one is laid. But silicone is not paint, and it has its own conditions for curing.

For its Trade Sanitary Silicone (data sheet, 2022), the manufacturer Soudal states that the surface skins over in about 7 minutes, while in depth the silicone cures at about 2 mm a day at 23 °C and 50% relative humidity. It may be applied at +5–+35 °C. Curing depends on temperature and humidity, and for other silicones the figures may differ. Source checked on 24 November 2026.

This means a joint 6 mm deep cures in about three days at room temperature, and longer in a cool room. The shower can be used only when the joint is cured through its whole depth, otherwise water washes it out or deforms it.

Around Ķekava there are garden society houses that are lived in all year, and some that stand unheated in winter. Parts of Dzērumi, for example Teika, Rožkalni and Radiotehnika, were originally summer-house cooperatives of company employees. If the bathroom in such a house is colder than +5 °C, renewing the joint has to be planned for when the room is heated and will stay warm until the silicone has cured.

The joint is twice as wide as it is deep

For its Trade Sanitary Silicone, Soudal gives a joint width of 5–30 mm, a minimum depth of 5 mm and a recommendation: the width is twice the depth. So a joint 10 mm wide between the tray and the tiles is about 5 mm deep, not a thin layer on the surface.

A thin joint tears away from the tile as soon as the tray or the wall moves a little. The same manufacturer states that this silicone does not stick to PE, PP and PTFE plastics, so it will not hold on such parts, however carefully it is applied.

Water under the tray: the joint or the trap

In its wet-room guidance, the City of Vaasa building control in Finland stresses that a shower cabin does not replace waterproofing. If water gets under the tray, it is important to work out which way it gets in: through the joint between the tray and the wall, or through the trap connection under the tray.

Replacing the cabin’s joint or seal will not stop this water if the trap connection is leaking, and the other way round. The signs are damp tile joints around the cabin, a skirting board that has come loose in the next room, or a stain on the neighbours' ceiling. Replacing the trap under a tray with no hatch requires lifting the tray or partly taking the cabin apart.

A repair with no single price: what the position of the fault decides

For fixing shower cabin leaks, the trap or the door seals, the price depends on where the fault is (tray, trap, door, panel), on whether spare parts are available and on whether the cabin has to be taken apart. Door seals and rollers are different for each manufacturer, and for an old model they are found by the profile and size of the old part.

If the tray has cracked, the repair can cost close to a new cabin. Then the price of shower cabin installation from €125 is useful for comparison.

About joints and a cold room

Water runs out between the closed doors. Are those the seals?

Most likely the vertical seals or magnetic strips that have hardened or slipped. They are replaced if a suitable profile exists. If the doors do not close all the way, the fault may also be in the rollers or hinges.

A garden house in Krogsils is cold in winter. When can the joints be renewed?

When the room is at least +5 °C and will stay warm until the silicone has cured. Soudal gives +5–+35 °C as the application range for its sanitary silicone. It is worth heating the room in good time so that the tiles and the tray are warm too, not just the air.

The gap between the tray and the wall is 12 mm. How deep should the joint be?

By Soudal’s recommendation the width is twice the depth, so for a joint 12 mm wide the depth is about 6 mm, and the minimum depth is 5 mm. By Soudal’s data, such a joint takes several days to cure at room temperature, because in depth the silicone cures at about 2 mm a day.
