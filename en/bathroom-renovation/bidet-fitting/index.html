The main technical question is not the fixing but the tap: if it has a hose with a hand shower that can end up below the water level in the bidet or WC, the supply needs protection against backflow. The tap and new outlets are not in the bidet price.

A hand shower that reaches the water level

Backflow is the moment when water in a pipe moves the opposite way: from a fixture back into the water main. If a hand shower head is lying in the bidet bowl or the WC under water at that moment, dirty water can be sucked into the pipe. That is why the supply that feeds such a shower needs valves that close the way back.

For example, the Swedish industry rules Säker Vatten 2026:1 (5.3.1–5.3.2), explaining the standard EN 1717, say this. If the hand shower of a shower or basin mixer tap reaches below the water level in a bidet or WC, you need a non-return valve and a vacuum valve, or two non-return valves. Another option is a shower handle with an ejector function, automatic closing and a non-return valve. For taps without a hose, an air gap of at least 20 mm is enough. In Latvia these rules are not binding; the text was viewed on 24 November 2026.

Non-return valve and vacuum valve
the non-return valve stops water flowing back, and the vacuum valve lets air in if low pressure appears in the pipe.
Two non-return valves in a row
if one does not seal, the other still holds the water back.
Handle with an ejector function
the shower closes by itself when you let go, and it has its own non-return valve.

When the pressure in the supply disappears

Backflow needs a moment when the pressure in the water main falls. That happens when there is a shut-off or works on the network. For example, on 10 November 2026 Ikšķiles māja, carrying out disinfection of the water tower in Ceplīši, warned of a possible interruption of water supply or reduced pressure. If at that moment a hand shower in some house was lying in a bidet with water, what is in the bowl can be sucked into a supply without a valve.

In a house with its own well the pressure falls when the well pump stops, for example in a power cut. So the protection is needed equally by a house on the network and a house with its own water.

Ķekava Municipality binding regulations No. 21/2022 (point 23) say this about a house fed from two independent water sources, its own well or borehole and the central water main. At the point where they meet there must be an air gap of at least 50 mm, or a special valve that creates such a gap. That is a different place from a bidet, but the same principle: the dirtier water must not get into the cleaner pipe. Text at likumi.lv viewed on 24 November 2026.

A bidet with the tap on the bowl and without a hose

On a classic bidet the tap sits on the edge of the bowl, and the tap spout is above the rim of the bowl. Water from the bowl cannot get into it: an air gap guards it. Such a bidet does not need a non-return valve set unless the tap has an extra hose.

If you choose a tap with a pull-out hose or a separate hand shower on the wall, one of the valve solutions above has to be included in the estimate. In some sets they are already built in; this can be seen in the tap’s data sheet.

The spot next to the WC and the outlets

A bidet needs a cold and a hot water supply and a drain outlet. If they were built in an earlier renovation, fitting the bidet means fixing it to the floor or wall and connecting. If there are no outlets, each new pipe is priced under the line “Supply or waste run to one fixture”. The bidet’s waste needs its own trap, and it is connected to the drain outlet just like a basin trap.

In a toilet room with a wall-hung WC, a wall-hung bidet can also go next to it on its own frame, if there is room in the wall. Then assembling the frame and boarding the wall are separate jobs, as in WC installation.

A bidet on ready outlets and a new run if there is none

Fitting a bidet next to the WC costs from €40 per bidet. It includes fixing the bidet and connecting it to the existing water and drain outlets; new runs and the tap are extra. A new run, if there is none, costs from €35 per run, with chasing and materials extra.

The price changes with the amount of work, access and materials: a floor-standing or wall-hung bidet, a tap with or without a hose, and whether the valve set is already built into the tap.

About connecting a bidet

The bidet tap has a built-in non-return valve. Is that enough?

If the shower can reach the water in the bowl, the Swedish Säker Vatten rules require, instead of a single non-return valve, a non-return valve together with a vacuum valve, or two non-return valves. The tap’s data sheet should say what protection it has.

What is an ejector handle?

It is a hand shower handle that closes by itself when you let go, and it has a non-return valve. The shower cannot stay open lying in the bowl, and water from the bowl does not return into it.

The house has its own well and a hydrophore. Is backflow possible there at all?

Yes. When the pump stops or the power goes, the pressure in the pipes falls just as it does during a shut-off on the network. The valve requirement for a hand shower at a bidet does not depend on where the water comes from.
