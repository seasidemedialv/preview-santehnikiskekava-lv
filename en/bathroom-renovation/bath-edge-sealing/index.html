Sealing a bath - silicone chosen by the bath's material

Sealing a bath in Ķekava means removing the old silicone joint and laying a new one between the bath and the wall.

The silicone has to be chosen to suit the surfaces: the manufacturer Bostik does not recommend its acid (acetoxy) sanitary silicone for acrylic, marble and several metals. The price is set by the length of the joint, removing the old silicone and mould, and whether the bath moves.

Acrylic, marble and galvanised steel: where acid silicone does not suit

Sanitary silicone comes in two main types: acid (acetoxy), which gives off acetic acid as it cures, and neutral. For its acetoxy silicone Zwaluw Silicone-Sanitary, Bostik (Den Braven) lists in the data sheet the surfaces it is not suitable for:

Surface What Bostik says about Zwaluw Silicone-Sanitary
Acrylic bath and tray (PMMA), polycarbonate (PC) not suitable; for plastic sanitary products it recommends the neutral Silicone-NO
Marble and concrete not suitable
Lead, copper, galvanised steel not suitable
PE, PP and soft plastics not suitable

This applies to this particular product, but the principle is general: the type of silicone is chosen by what the joint sticks to on both sides. An acrylic bath next to a tiled wall needs a silicone that the manufacturer names for acrylic; a bath next to natural stone finishing needs one that suits stone. If acrylic and marble meet in one joint, the silicone has to suit both.

For plastic sanitary products Bostik recommends its neutral Silicone-NO. Whether a given silicone is acetoxy or neutral is stated in its data sheet; the word “sanitary” on the tube does not tell you that on its own.

Joint depth against width and the gap along an acrylic bath

For joints up to 10 mm, Bostik recommends for this silicone a depth to width ratio of 1:1, at least 5 mm. So a joint 6 mm wide is also about 6 mm deep, not a thin film across the gap. Thin silicone tears as soon as the bath moves a little.

The gap is set by the bath and the wall. The manufacturer Riho allows gaps along the perimeter of its acrylic baths of up to 5 mm, to be filled with sanitary silicone or sealing tape. If the gap is much wider, the joint no longer makes up for it: then the wall is not straight or the bath is too small for the space, and that is put right with finishing work.

Bostik does not recommend its acetoxy silicone for PE and PP plastics either, so on PE or PP parts the joint does not hold. If there are such parts at the edge of the bath, the joint is planned so that the silicone does not touch them.

Before the new joint: a bath that does not move

If the bath stands on feet that are not adjusted, or on a loose frame, a new joint on it will tear away just like the old one, so the bath is checked before the joint and, if needed, fixed in place. For steel enamel baths the manufacturer Kaldewei requires the bath to be loaded before the joint; more on that on the bath replacement page.

Renewing the joint: what the amount of work depends on

If after a new joint water still appears on the floor by the bath or at the neighbours below, the fault may be in the waste or the overflow under the bath, not in the joint. Then the trap needs checking through the panel. If there is already damp or mould under the bath, it is removed before the new joint, otherwise it stays under the silicone.

In a block of flats in central Ķekava or in Baloži, the first sign can be a stain on the neighbour’s ceiling under the bath. Then you first have to understand whether the water comes through the joint, through the waste or from a pipe in the wall, and only then decide about the joint. If a neighbour has already been flooded, see the page flat flooded from above.

If the bath is old and the joint cracks after every renewal, it is worth considering replacing the bath: a new bath on correct supports moves less.

About silicone and the joint

How can I tell what silicone was in the old joint?

Fresh acid silicone smells of vinegar, but once cured it is hard to tell by smell. If the bath is acrylic, the old joint has to be removed down to a clean surface in any case, and the new silicone must be one that the manufacturer names for acrylic.

The joint cracks every year in the same place. Why?

It points to movement at exactly that spot: the bath settles under weight there, or the joint is too thin. Then what helps is fixing the bath in place, or a thicker joint according to the silicone’s data sheet, not just a new layer.

There is a 2 cm gap between the bath and the wall. Is it filled with silicone?

Riho allows gaps along the perimeter of acrylic baths of up to 5 mm, and Bostik recommends for its silicone, for joints up to 10 mm, a depth equal to the width. A gap of two centimetres at an acrylic bath means the bath is too small for the space or the wall is not straight, and that is put right with tiles or finishing, not with a larger amount of silicone.
