In a private house this is needed, for example, when a bathroom is fitted out in an attic or on the second storey, where there was no water or sewer before. The outlet positions are decided before waterproofing and tiles, because later they can be changed only by opening the floor or the wall.

An upstairs bathroom: the drain has to get down

If a house has sewer and water only on the ground floor, a second-storey or attic bathroom needs them built from scratch. The upper floor’s drain has to reach the existing sewer below, and the water pipe has to climb up. Both run along a wall or a shaft, and the spot is chosen so that there is room on the way for the fall of the drain.

For example, the Swedish rules Säker Vatten 2026:1 require the compartment of a concealed cistern to be on the warm side of the structure, where it does not freeze. Supply pipes in an attic are also run on the warm side: along an inner wall, not along an outer wall or through a cold roof space. If that is not possible, the pipes need insulation.

In the floor only drain and floor-drain holes

For example, the Swedish industry rules Säker Vatten 2026:1 (4.6.1) say that the floor of a bath or shower room may have only holes for waste pipes and the floor drain, and in the shower or bath spot itself only the drain. A waste pipe must be at least 60 mm from the waterproofing of the adjoining wall and at least 200 mm from the drain’s outer flange (500 mm with a plastic floor covering). This is a Swedish industry document, not a Latvian standard; viewed on 24 November 2026.

In practice this means: water supply pipes come from the wall, not through the floor, and only the waste for the WC, basin or bath goes through the floor, away from the wall corner and the drain. The fewer holes in the waterproofing, the fewer places water can seep out. This layout is decided while the floor is still open.

The frame and the manifold on the upper storey

If there will be a wall-hung WC upstairs, Säker Vatten 2026:1 allows its frame in a room with a waterproofed floor to be screwed only to the wall, not to the floor, so as not to damage the floor waterproofing. So the place for the frame needs a wall or a framework that holds it, and this has to be known before the waste and water runs are laid.

If the second storey also has an underfloor heating manifold, the same rules (4.5.1–4.5.2) put it in a prefabricated cabinet with a watertight bottom, raised at least 50 mm at the side walls, and a leak must come out in a room where people usually are and notice it easily. The manifold’s place near the bathroom is decided together with the plan of the outlets.

The run price line, and moving a WC with no single price

A supply or waste run to one fixture: cold, hot or drain costs from €35 per run. The price line applies to each pipe separately: a basin usually needs three (cold water, hot water and drain), a WC two. Chasing and materials are extra, and the price changes with the amount of work, access and materials.

Moving a WC to a new spot has no single price: it is set by the distance to the soil stack and the fall, opening the floor or wall, and whether a pump (Sololift type) is needed. In a block of flats the WC stays by the building’s common soil stack, and usually it cannot be moved far. If the WC will be on a frame, its installation is described on the page WC installation; building a whole bathroom from scratch is a separate price line on the page bathroom renovation.

About new outlets

The attic bathroom will be under a roof slope. How do I protect the pipes from frost?

The pipes are run along the warm side of an inner wall, not along an outer wall or through a cold roof space. If that is not possible, the pipes need insulation, and a cistern is not placed in the wall on the cold side.

Can the frame of a wall-hung WC upstairs be fixed to the floor?

If the floor is waterproofed, the Swedish Säker Vatten rules do not recommend it: the frame is screwed only to the wall, so that the screws do not puncture the waterproofing. If the frame cannot be fixed to the wall, a framework is needed first.

The bathroom only needs a new outlet for a washing machine. Is that this job too?

Yes, that is one or two runs: water and drain. If they already exist and the machine is only connected, that is a washing machine hook-up job.
