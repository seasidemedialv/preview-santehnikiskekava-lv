A running WC can let through up to 400 litres of water a day, and on the Ķekavas nami bill you pay for this water twice: for water supply and for sewerage. How much that is in money depends on which tariff zone the house is in. A cistern hidden in the wall has its own page.

A running cistern on the Ķekavas nami bill

The British water-saving organisation Waterwise estimates that a running WC can waste up to 400 litres of water a day. That is the upper limit, when the cistern runs all the time. Multiplied by the tariffs published by SIA “Ķekavas nami”, this gives the following approximate figures (excluding VAT, our calculation):

Tariff zone Water, €/m³ Sewerage, €/m³ 400 litres a day, approx.
Katlakalns, Krustkalni, Ķekava, Odukalns, Rāmava, Valdlauči, Ziedonis, Vimbukrogs and Daugmale village 1.09 3.04 approx. €1.65 a day, about €50 a month
Baldone and Baldone parish 1.68 2.96 approx. €1.86 a day, about €56 a month
Baloži 0.59 1.60 approx. €0.88 a day, about €26 a month

The monthly figure assumes 30 days and 12 m³ of water. A small trickle down the side of the bowl lets through less, but it too runs without a break until the valve is repaired, and the meter counts it just the same.

Why three zones give three figures

The main tariff zone is a closed list. In the notice that Ķekavas nami published on 27 November 2025 (OP 2025/229.DA44), it applies to the villages of Ķekava parish Katlakalns, Krustkalni, Ķekava, Odukalns, Rāmava, Valdlauči, Ziedonis and Vimbukrogs, and to Daugmale village in Daugmale parish. The notice explains the tariff change partly by the increase in the water supply and wastewater tariffs of SIA “Rīgas ūdens”.

Baloži has its own, lower tariff: on 27 November 2025 SPRK approved for Baloži water management €0.59/m³ for water supply and €1.60/m³ for sewerage, for 2026–2027. Since 1 September 2026 Baložu komunālā saimniecība has been merged into Ķekavas nami, but the tariff in Baloži stays its own. For Baldone and Baldone parish there is a third tariff row on the Ķekavas nami website.

If a house is in a village not named in these lists, for example Krogsils, ask Ķekavas nami or your own water supplier about the water charge and the network. A house with its own well and septic tank has no water bill, but the water from a running cistern still ends up in the septic tank.

An exposed cistern or a cistern in the wall

In an exposed cistern whose lid can be taken off, two parts can let water into the WC: the fill valve, which has to shut when the float rises, or the flush mechanism seal at the bottom of the cistern. In both cases the price is the same: from €35 per job, parts extra.

If the WC is wall-hung and the cistern is hidden in the wall, access is only through the flush-plate opening, and that is a different job: concealed WC repair.

The row from €35 and when it no longer applies

Fixing a WC cistern that keeps running costs from €35 per job. It includes replacing the fill valve or the flush mechanism of an exposed cistern; parts extra. The price changes with the amount of work, access and materials: for example, whether the cistern is on the WC or high on the wall, and whether suitable parts are available for it.

If parts for the old cistern can no longer be obtained, the job is priced by the row for WC installation.

Questions about a running WC

The WC runs quietly, nothing can be heard. How do I spot it?

Wipe the inside wall of the bowl dry under the rim and after a while see whether water is running down it again. You can also put a coloured liquid in the cistern: if colour appears in the bowl without flushing, one of the valves is leaking. A damp streak that does not dry is the same sign.

In an old house the cistern is high on the wall, with a chain. Do you repair those too?

Yes, it is an exposed cistern, so the same rate, from €35, fits. The mechanism there is different from a low cistern on the WC, and its parts are harder to find. If there are none, the cistern is replaced with a new one or a new WC with its own cistern is fitted.

Water runs over the edge of the cistern onto the floor. Is that the same fault?

It means the fill valve does not shut and the overflow cannot cope or is blocked. Close the valve on the cistern supply and do not flush the toilet. If the water has already reached the neighbours or got into the floor, see the page on water leaks.
