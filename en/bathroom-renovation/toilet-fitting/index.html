A floor-standing WC with fitting, sealing and connection costs from €60, a frame with a wall-hung WC from €85 (without wall boarding). If the old WC has a big cistern with no dual-flush button, the new one lets through two to three times less water in one flush, and in a house with its own well or septic tank you can feel it.

Flush volume: the old cistern against the dual-flush button

The Consumer Council for Water (CCW) in Britain states that older WCs let through up to 13 litres in one flush, while WCs with two buttons use 4–6 litres. The difference arises every time someone presses the button, so in a family of four it adds up every day.

In Ķekava Municipality this figure means different things depending on how the house gets its water. In a house connected to the network of SIA “Ķekavas nami”, every cubic metre appears on the bill twice: in both the water and the sewerage tariff. In a house with its own well, every litre of flushing first has to be pumped in, and in a house with a septic tank it then ends up in the tank, which has to be emptied.

When you buy a new WC, the data sheet or the packaging gives the flush volume in litres and whether the button is dual-flush. If the cistern is separate from the WC, both parts must be from one set.

The frame for a concealed WC: screws into the wall, not the floor

A concealed, or wall-hung, WC hangs on an installation frame that is hidden behind the wall boarding together with the flush cistern. Our price from €85 includes mounting the frame, connecting it to the water and the drain, and hanging the WC on a finished wall. Plasterboard and tiles in front of the frame are a tiler’s job and are not in the price.

For example, the Swedish industry rules Säker Vatten 2026:1 (4.2.3 and 4.7.1) say that in a room with a waterproofed floor the frame of a wall-hung WC is screwed only to the wall, not to the floor, so as not to damage the floor waterproofing. The compartment of a concealed cistern must be on the warm side of the structure, where it does not freeze. This is not a Latvian standard, but a good example of how to position the frame. Source checked on 24 November 2026.

In a private house this matters if the toilet is by an outer wall or in an attic under the roof. A cistern with water is not put on the side of the wall that cools in winter, and the frame is not fixed with holes in the floor if the floor is already waterproofed.

What must be in place before the WC is connected

  • For a floor-standing WC: a finished floor with tiles and the drain outlet exactly where the WC will stand.
  • For a frame: a wall or a framework to fix it to, and a water supply at the height of the frame’s cistern.
  • The WC, the seat and, in the case of a frame, the flush plate: they are not in the price, they are bought separately.
  • If the old WC has to be removed, that is a separate job, not included in the WC price from €60.

For a floor-standing WC, sealing means two places: the WC outlet connection to the drain socket, which must let through neither water nor smell, and the joint between the WC base and the floor. If the new WC’s outlet does not go straight into the socket, a flexible connector or a bend that adjusts the direction and the distance is put between them.

If the WC moves to another wall or into another room, it is no longer just installation: a new drain and water run is needed. This work is described on the page building plumbing outlets. In a block of flats where the risers are being replaced at the same time, it is worth coordinating the WC work with the riser pipe replacement, because the height of the new branch can differ from the old one.

A floor-standing WC, a frame and a run: three separate rows

“Floor-standing WC: fitting, sealing, connection” costs from €60 per WC. “Concealed frame with a wall-hung WC (no wall boarding)” costs from €85 per set. If a new pipe has to be built to the WC’s spot, “Supply or waste run to one fixture: cold, hot or drain” costs from €35 per run, with chasing and materials extra.

The final sum changes with the amount of work, access and materials: for example, whether the old WC is cemented into the floor, whether the drain socket is in an old cast-iron pipe, and whether the frame needs the water supply adjusted. All the rows are on the prices page.

About replacing a WC in Ķekava houses

We will put a frame in the attic under sloping ceilings. What has to be taken into account?

The frame needs enough height for the cistern and the flush plate, and the cistern must be on the warm side of the wall. The Swedish Säker Vatten rules require this precisely so that the cistern compartment does not freeze. If the WC in the attic is new, a drain run to the sewer on the first floor is needed as well.

The Ķekavas nami bill has two lines for water. Does less flushing reduce both?

Yes, because the water and sewerage tariffs are charged on the same cubic metres that the water meter shows. Every litre the WC does not use is less in both lines.

The old WC is cemented into the floor. How does that affect the job?

Removing a WC that is cemented in is a separate job, not in the WC installation price from €60. After that the floor under the new WC has to be level. If the tiles under the old WC are damaged, repairing them is a tiler’s job.
