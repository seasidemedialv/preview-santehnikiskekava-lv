Hauling away the old bath, a cast-iron bath and a bath panel are charged separately. For acrylic and enamelled steel baths the manufacturers require different procedures, and whether the joint at the wall holds depends on them.

Tiles 10 cm below the rim and a bath that can be taken out

In the installation instructions for its acrylic baths, the manufacturer Riho starts with what has to be done before the bath is brought into the room. The water and sewer outlets must be built before the bath is put in place, and tiles or plaster on the wall must reach 10 cm below the rim of the bath. Then the joint between the bath and the wall rests against a tile, not against an unfinished wall.

Riho’s second requirement concerns the future: after installation the bath must remain removable without building work. This means the bath is not walled in so that it can be taken out only by breaking the tiles. The bath stands on Riho supports, and gaps along the perimeter, no wider than 5 mm, are filled with sanitary silicone or sealing tape once the waste has been checked.

If the old bath was walled in, after it is taken out the wall and the floor under it have to be put in order before the new one is brought in, and that is a tiler’s job. For acrylic baths from other manufacturers, see their instructions; the figures and supports may differ.

Enamelled steel: a bath with weight while the joint cures

For its steel enamel baths and shower trays, the manufacturer Kaldewei requires the bath to be loaded before the flexible joint, for example by filling it with water, and the weight not to be removed until the joint has cured completely. An empty bath and a bath with water and a person are at different heights, and a joint cured with the bath loaded stays in the position the bath is used in.

Kaldewei also states that mortar or adhesive must not touch the surface under the rim and that the enamel must not come into contact with metal. Between the rim and the wall the manufacturer recommends a sound-insulation tape, for example WES 130. It reduces the hollow sound when water runs into a steel bath.

A new bath of another size or in another place

If the new bath is the same size and stays in the old place, the main work is the connection of the waste and the overflow to the existing outlet. In a private house the new bath may be longer or corner-shaped, and then the outlet positions change: they have to be moved before the bath is brought into the room, and that is a job of building plumbing outlets.

The trap under the bath needs access through a panel or a hatch; the panel is not in the bath installation price. Which silicone to choose for the joint between the new bath and the wall is described on the bath sealing page.

The bath row from €60 and a cast-iron bath priced separately

Fitting a steel or acrylic bath and connecting the waste costs from €60 per bath. It includes setting the bath on feet or a frame and connecting the waste and the overflow. Hauling away the old bath, fitting a cast-iron bath and the panel are priced on top.

Taking a cast-iron bath out of a flat is a separate job, and its price is set by access: the storey, the stairwell and the door width. The price also changes with the amount of work and materials, for example a new waste and overflow set or the sound-insulation tape Kaldewei recommends.

Questions about a new bath

Can the new bath be tiled in all round so the feet are not seen?

It can be closed off with a panel, but Riho requires for its acrylic baths that after installation the bath can be removed without building work. So the panel has to be removable or have a hatch, not walled in together with the rim of the bath.

The new bath is shorter than the old one. What happens to the wall at the end?

Riho requires the tiles to reach 10 cm below the rim of the bath. If an untiled strip is left by a shorter bath, it has to be tiled before the joint, and that is a tiler’s job, not plumbing.

The old trap and overflow. Are new ones fitted together with the bath?

Connecting the waste and the overflow is in the bath installation price, but the set itself is material. If the old set is plastic and cracked, or does not fit the new bath’s holes, a new one is fitted.
