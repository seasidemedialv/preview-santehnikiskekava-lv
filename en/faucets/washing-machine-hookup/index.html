In a kitchen leaks most often start in the sink unit, where the dishwasher connection usually is too: the material of the Swedish industry organisation Säker Vatten says that more than 70% of kitchen water damage starts in the sink unit or right next to it. That is why the most important things are a valve you can turn off without pulling the machine out, and a hose that is sealed properly.

A valve with a handle above the worktop

When a hose or its connection starts to leak, the first job is to turn off the water at the machine. If the valve is behind a built-in dishwasher or deep in the cabinet, it cannot be reached while the water is already on the floor.

For example, in Sweden the industry rules Säker Vatten (2016) say that the supply to a dishwasher must have a visible, easily accessible shut-off valve whose handle is above the worktop. In a kitchen with a built-in machine this means the water can be turned off without taking anything apart. By the same principle, for a washing machine in a basement or utility room it is convenient to put the valve in the wall above the machine, not behind it.

The hose also has to stay free on a built-in machine. The manufacturer Miele states for built-in dishwashers (North American installation diagram): if the inlet hose stays coiled under the machine, the height of the recess must be at least 835 mm (885 mm for XXL models), and the hose must not be pinched by the machine, bent or laid next to hot water pipes.

Säker Vatten is a Swedish industry standard, not a Latvian requirement; the Miele diagram applies to the specific models. Checked on 24 November 2026.

The flat washer in the hose nut

In the same Säker Vatten material, the list of the most common causes of kitchen leaks begins with a dishwasher hose connection without a flat washer. The flat washer is a gasket inside the hose nut, and without it the connection can let water through.

So when a machine is connected, the washers in the hose nuts are checked, and if they are missing or have hardened, new ones are fitted. The other causes from the same list, tap supply pipes, the trap transition and a corrugated hose, are described on the kitchen sink page.

Waste: a trap with a branch or a standpipe

The machine’s waste is usually taken either to a branch on the sink trap or to a standpipe in the wall. For appliance traps of 1½ inch with a 75 mm water seal, the manufacturer McAlpine supplies a plug: while there is no machine, the trap works as an ordinary sink trap, and the branch can be opened later.

The hose goes into the standpipe trap at the height given by the machine’s manufacturer, and the figures differ between manufacturers. For example, LG (a support page in the UK) puts the drain hose of a washing machine 40–100 cm from the floor, because outside this range the water can drain out of the drum by itself. If in a basement the waste is higher than the machine can lift the water, another solution is needed; more on the basement page.

The machine line, a new supply run and a fridge

Water and drain hook-up for a washing machine or dishwasher costs from €35 per machine. It includes connecting to the existing water tap and waste.

If there is no tap or waste where the machine goes, or the valve has to be moved to a place that can be reached, each new pipe is charged as the line Supply or waste run to one fixture: cold, hot or drain, from €35 per run. A cold water supply for a fridge with an ice maker is charged the same way; for it the manufacturer Samsung gives a pressure of 20–125 PSI. The price changes with the amount of work (a built-in machine in a recess or a free-standing one), access (basement, cabinet) and materials: valve, hoses and a trap with a branch.

About machines and pressure

The dishwasher valve is behind the machine. Can it be moved?

It can. The valve is moved to a place that can be reached, for example in the neighbouring cabinet or above the worktop, as Säker Vatten provides for in Sweden. If a new pipe has to be run for it, it is charged as the line Supply or waste run to one fixture: cold, hot or drain, from €35 per run.

The trap has a branch for a machine, but we will fit the machine only later. Does the branch get in the way?

If the branch is closed with a plug, it does not. McAlpine supplies a plug with its appliance traps for exactly this reason: without a machine the trap works as an ordinary sink trap. When the machine is there, the plug is taken off and the drain hose connected.

The ice maker of the fridge fills slowly. How can the water supply be checked?

Samsung suggests a simple check: under normal conditions a 170 ml paper cup fills from the fridge’s water supply in about 10 seconds. If it fills much more slowly, the pressure has to be checked, and whether the supply hose is kinked. Samsung also says that below 20 PSI a pressure booster pump can be fitted.
