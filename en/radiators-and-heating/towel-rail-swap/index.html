In a new private house near Ķekava where the rooms are heated only by the floor, it is worth knowing before you buy how warm the water reaching the rail will be. That decides more than the shape or colour of the rail.

Floor circuit temperature and a water towel rail

In floor circuits the water is cool. The manufacturer Roth, for example, allows a flow temperature of no more than 55 °C for its underfloor heating, because the average temperature in the screed in the pipe zone must not be higher. A water towel rail connected to the same water cannot be warmer than what the floor receives.

So in the data sheet of the new rail look at the water temperature at which its output is stated, and compare it with your floor flow temperature. If the difference is large, the rail will warm towels poorly however big it is. The floor temperature is set by the heat source settings, and its supplier knows them.

This question is best settled before the screed and tiles, because then it can still be decided where the pipes to the rail will come from. Once the tiles are laid, each new pipe means opening the tiles.

Fixing it to a tiled or plasterboard wall

If the new rail is taller or wider than the old one, it needs new fixing points in the wall. In a tiled wall they have to be drilled through the tiles, and first you need to know where the pipes run in the wall. In a plasterboard wall a heavier rail needs a fixing in the frame or in a built-in plate.

In a new build where the walls are not yet boarded, the fixing place can be planned at once: a plate or a cross-member is put in the frame exactly where the rail’s brackets will be. Later it cannot be done without opening the boarding.

Pipes in the wall and the shower zone

If the rail is moved, new pipes will be run in the wall. For example, the Swedish industry rules Säker Vatten advise running the water pipe up to a fixture without joints, so that no spots are left in the wall that could start to seep. The same rules treat the wall up to a height of 2.0 m behind a shower or bath as part of the shower or bath zone, and allow only the mixer tap and shower connections in the wall there. That is a good reason not to take the rail’s pipes through the shower wall.

Säker Vatteninstallation 2026:1 and SBUF 13222 (2016) are Swedish industry rules, not a Latvian standard; checked 24 November 2026.

In a block of flats the rail may be connected to the building’s riser. Then replacing it is usually agreed with the building manager, because under Article 4 of the Apartment Property Law, heating elements that work off the building’s shared engineering lines belong to the common property; how this is set in your building is shown by the owners' decisions.

Replacement on the same pipes or a new pipe

Replacing a heated towel rail in the bathroom on the same pipes costs from €80: taking off the old rail and attaching the new one to the existing pipes. If the rail moves to another wall, each new run to the fixture position costs from €35, and chasing and materials are not in the price.

The price is affected by access (a rail behind a washing machine or in a narrow niche), the wall material, the amount of work and materials. In the form write what the rail is connected to, how tall and wide it is and what the wall is like, and describe in words how the existing rail is connected.

About the towel rail

Can a water towel rail be connected to the underfloor heating manifold?

It can, if the manifold has a free outlet, but the rail will receive the same cool water as the floor and will warm poorly. If you need dry towels in the bathroom, another connection or an electric rail has to be considered.

The old chrome rail is rusting at the joints. Can only they be replaced?

If the rail itself is sound and only the joint leaks, it can be resealed or replaced: sealing a dripping joint that is reachable costs from €35. If the rust is in the pipe of the rail itself, it is better to replace the rail.

We already have underfloor heating built. Does it make sense to fit a water towel rail?

If the rail can be connected only to the floor water, it will receive water as cool as the floor’s, in a Roth system no warmer than 55 °C. First find out your floor flow temperature and compare it with the rail’s data; then it will be clear whether it will dry towels.
