Floor, radiators or a flat's riser: where to start

In a new private house in Ķekava, Baloži or Katlakalns the heating may be in the floor only. Then the most important part happens before the screed: installing water underfloor heating with a pressure test and a circuit diagram. If years later some zone stays cold, underfloor heating repair starts at the manifold.

If a garden society house in Krogsils, Rāmava or Valdlauči has become a home for the whole year, its heating circuit may need extending or building from scratch. Then radiator replacement and installation helps, and after the rebuild heating system flushing, so that no building debris is left in the new circuit. If the circuit stands over winter with antifreeze, the page about winter draining covers it.

In a flat with the building’s central heating, the radiator and the heated towel rail are usually connected to the building’s riser, so the work starts with the building manager. For a dripping valve or a radiator that collects air there is radiator repair.

A new heat source on an old circuit

A house’s heating can be rebuilt in parts: first the heating boiler is replaced or a heat pump is fitted, while the radiators and pipes stay the same. The building code has its own point for exactly this moment. Point 15.3 of LBN 231-15 provides that in new buildings, where technically possible and economically justified, temperature regulation is installed in each room or zone with hydraulic balancing, and the same applies to an existing building in which the heat generator is replaced.

LBN 231-15 “Heating and ventilation of residential and public buildings” (Cabinet Regulation No. 310), point 15.3 as worded by Cabinet Regulation No. 362 of 30 June 2026, and point 13; likumi.lv, checked 24 November 2026.

In practice this means: if the radiators have old manual valves or no valves at all, the question of thermostatic valves and balancing the circuit has to be settled in the same project in which the heat source changes, not after the first winter. This is pipe and valve work that can be done separately from fitting the heat source itself.

If the new heat source runs at a low temperature, the behaviour of the water in the circuit changes too. The manufacturer Spirotech states that in heat pump systems with a flow temperature of 40 °C or lower, microorganisms can grow and form a biofilm. What this looks like in floor circuits is described on the page about underfloor heating repair.

A filter on the return, before the water goes back to the heating boiler

The manufacturer ADEY advises placing its MagnaClean TwinTech magnetic filter on the return, between the last radiator and the heating boiler. All the water coming back from the rooms passes through the filter there. The filter holds back on average a year’s quantity of particles, so it is cleaned once a year, and in systems with a lot of sludge or without prior flushing more often in the first year.

So a filter does not replace flushing. If a new heat source is connected to an old circuit, the deposits are first taken out of the circuit, and the filter afterwards catches what comes loose later. Fitting a filter on the return pipe is pipe work and does not touch the heating boiler or heat pump itself.

Questions about a house's heating circuit

The heating boiler will be fitted by its supplier. Can you build the pipes and radiators?

Yes, installing heating pipes and radiators in a private house can be done separately from the heat source. The position of the heating boiler or heat pump has to be known before the route is planned, because the pipe length depends on it. The unit itself is fitted by its supplier.

Can a magnetic filter be fitted to a system that has run for several years?

It can; the filter goes on the return pipe before the heating boiler. ADEY states that in systems with a lot of sludge or without prior flushing the filter has to be cleaned more often in the first year, so in an old circuit it is worth considering flushing first.

We are changing the heating boiler but keeping the radiators. Do they need thermostatic valves?

Point 15.3 of LBN 231-15 provides, for an existing building in which the heat generator is replaced, temperature regulation in each room or zone with hydraulic balancing, where technically possible and economically justified. So valves and balancing are worth planning together with the new heat source. Replacing the radiators themselves is not required.
