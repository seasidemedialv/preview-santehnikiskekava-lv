On some valves the insert or the seal can be replaced without draining the circuit, but after the work the valve setting has to be put back as it was, so that other radiators do not get cooler. There is no single price for fixing a valve, a thermostatic head or a leak: it is set by where the fault is, whether the system has to be drained and the spare part.

A valve with a replaceable insert: work without draining the circuit

Not every valve repair means an empty system. For example, the data sheet of the Heimeier Calypso exact valve says the whole thermostatic insert can be replaced with a special assembly tool without draining the heating system. The same data sheet states that the spindle has a double O-ring seal and the outer O-ring can be replaced under pressure, so a drip at the spindle does not always mean a new valve.

This applies to particular valve types. In an old house the valve may have no marking or no replaceable insert, and then whether the circuit can be left full is decided on site from the valve body. If the whole valve has to be replaced, the circuit or part of it is drained.

Heimeier Calypso exact data sheet (distributed by akvedukts.lv); a manufacturer’s document for a particular valve, not a general rule. Checked 24 November 2026.

Before taking a radiator off: the turns of the return screw

If a radiator has to be taken off for the repair, it is shut off on both sides. For the Heimeier Regutec return screw the manual says to close it with a 5 mm hexagon key and, if the screw was set for hydraulic balance, to count the turns while closing. Then after the radiator is put back the screw can be opened the same number of turns and the earlier setting restored.

The same applies to a new thermostatic valve. The pre-setting of the Calypso exact can be chosen steplessly from 1 to 8, the factory setting is 8, and it is changed with a key or a 13 mm spanner. If the new valve stays on the factory setting but the old one was turned down, the radiator may take more heat than before, and the radiators further along the circuit warm more weakly.

A whistling valve and a dirty circuit

If a thermostatic valve whistles or hisses, the cause may be too large a pressure drop across it. The Calypso exact data sheet says that for quiet operation the pressure drop across thermostatic valves should, by experience, not exceed about 0.2 bar (20 kPa). Then the pump setting has to be looked at too, not only the valve.

In an old system a new valve can clog as fast as the old one. The IMI Heimeier Eclipse pack sheet warns that before thermostatic valves are replaced in a heavily contaminated old system it should be flushed. There is more on this on the page heating system flushing.

An aluminium radiator in a circuit with copper pipes

If the circuit has aluminium radiators, the heating water has a narrower margin. For example, in Sentinel’s technical sheet the pH suitable for aluminium is from 7.0 to 8.5, and corrosion speeds up both below 6.0 and above 9.0. The same sheet warns that dissolved copper can settle on aluminium and form a micro-galvanic cell; the accepted limit in untreated system water is 0.1 mg/l of copper.

In a house with copper pipes and brass valves this means: if an aluminium radiator starts to leak from its body, the heating water has to be looked at too, not only one joint. This is a manufacturer’s water chemistry note, not a Latvian standard.

A slow leak at a valve and the policy

A dripping radiator valve is easy to put off, because the water comes drop by drop and the heating boiler still holds the pressure. A policy may not treat such a case as an emergency: in the BALTA extended-risk terms (No. 1202.09) the exclusions include wear of seals and long-lasting leaks, and point 8.9 of the ERGO Premium terms excludes renewing a heating system if the cause is wear or rust.

A valve that drips for months leaves on the floor exactly the damage these terms do not cover. If a damp patch has appeared under the radiator, photograph it and fill in the form.

BALTA No. 1202.09 and ERGO Premium (in force from 1 December 2025) terms read on 24 November 2026; the conditions in other policies may differ.

Repair priced by the place of the fault, replacement from €75

The price of fixing a radiator valve, thermostatic head or leak is set by where the fault is, whether the system has to be drained and the spare part. For bleeding the circuit and topping up the pressure the price is set by the number of radiators and whether the expansion vessel has to be checked. About repairing a radiator

It drips at the valve spindle, under the thermostatic head. Does the whole valve have to be replaced?

Not always. If the valve’s spindle seal is replaceable, as in Heimeier Calypso exact valves, where the outer O-ring can be changed under pressure, the seal is enough. Whether your valve is like this is seen on site; an old valve without a replaceable seal is replaced whole.

After a valve was replaced, one radiator is hot but the farthest one in the room is cool. Why?

Possibly the new valve was left on the factory setting. For Calypso exact valves it is 8, the highest. If the old valve was turned down, the pre-setting has to be put back so that the circuit again shares the heat as before.

The valve has been dripping since autumn. Will the policy cover the damaged floor?

Not necessarily. In the BALTA and ERGO terms a slow leak, wear of seals and rust are among the exclusions, so a slowly damaged floor may stay your responsibility. Read the exclusions of your own policy and fix the valve before the patch grows.
