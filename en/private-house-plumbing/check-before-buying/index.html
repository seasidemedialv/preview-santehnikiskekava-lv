Plumbing check before buying a house - pipe age and what an inspection shows

A plumbing check before selling or buying in the Ķekava area is about what the buyer takes over along with the keys but the papers do not show: how old the pipes are, how a private well meets mains water, where the basement waste pipe goes and where the meter stands.

An inspection can show some of this; the rest needs a camera or the seller’s documents.

Pipe age and the home policy

For a buyer, the age of the pipes is not only a repair question. For example, the terms of the If home policy (No. TPI-S-20243-LV) say that the repair of a leaking pipe or other utility line itself is covered only if it is not older than 10 years: in full up to 5 years, for half the sum up to 10 years. For an older pipe, If may cover the consequences of a leak, but not the repair of the pipe itself; the exact position is set by the policy.

That is why it is worth asking the seller when the water pipes and waste pipes were replaced and whether there is an invoice, an act or a diagram. If there is no answer, assume that the pipes are older than 10 years and assess their condition at the inspection: the material, the joints and traces of leaks. The terms in your policy may be different, so read its exclusions before the deal as well.

What an inspection shows by eye

Part of what the Ķekava Municipality water management rules require of houses on the network can be seen at an inspection:

A well next to mains water
if the house has both sources, the connection point must have an air gap of 50 mm or more, a dedicated non-return valve, or a removable insert that is taken out whenever the other supply is used (point 23).
Basement waste pipe
fixtures below the level of the nearest inspection chamber cover must be on a separate line with an electrically operated gate valve with a signal sensor, or a special one-way valve (point 24).
Rainwater
whether the roof downpipes or the drainage pump outlet do not run into the sewer inspection chamber, because that is prohibited (point 49).
Inside the house
what material the pipes are made of, whether the joints show traces of leaks, how old the water heater is and whether its safety valve works, and whether the valves close.

Ķekava Municipality binding regulations No. 21/2022, points 23, 24, 49 and 60; compared on likumi.lv on 24 November 2026. If terms No. TPI-S-20243-LV, in force from 24 September 2024.

The meter assembly and summer pipes

At an inspection it is also worth seeing the meter assembly: whether it is in the house, the basement or a manhole in the yard, whether it is dry there and whether the seals are intact. What the contract terms require of the assembly and who pays for a damaged meter is on the page yearly plumbing check for a house.

In former garden societies, for example Ziedonis, Mellupi or Jenči, it must be checked separately whether the water service pipe and the pipes were meant for summer only. More about this on the page garden house water supply all year.

A sewer outlet with no diagram

If a sewer outlet has no as-built documentation, point 60 of the Ķekava regulations requires, when the owner changes, a CCTV inspection of the pipes at the owner’s expense. It can also be done before the deal, and then the faults found become part of the negotiation. In the recording you can see whether the pipe has sagged, whether roots have grown into it and whether the joints have opened.

Where the supply is a private well or borehole, the quality of its water cannot be judged by eye: it needs a laboratory analysis, which is worth ordering before the deal.

Inspection and video check: what sets the sum

There is no single price for an inspection before a purchase. It is set by the size of the house, whether a sewer video check is needed and whether a written opinion is needed. The price of a video check is set by the length of the pipe and access through an inspection point or manhole, whether the pipe has to be flushed first, and whether a recording showing the damaged spot is needed.

Questions: Plumbing check before buying a house

The seller says the house is connected to the Ķekavas nami network. How can I check?

A house on the network has a service contract with SIA “Ķekavas nami” and a meter installed and sealed by the service provider. Ask for the contract and look at the meter assembly. If the water comes from a well and the waste goes to a septic tank, there will be no contract, but the system must be in the municipal register.

The seller does not know when the pipes were replaced. What does this mean for the buyer?

Because of the policy conditions, it is safer to treat the pipes as old. For example, under If’s terms the repair of the pipe itself is covered only up to the age of 10 years. An inspection can show the material and the joints but not the exact age, so in the price and the contract it is worth allowing for replacing the pipes.

In winter the house stands empty and the water has been drained. Can it still be checked?

Yes, but only in part. To see leaks and test the pressure, there must be water in the system, so filling it has to be agreed with the seller before the inspection. A sewer video check does not need water in the system if there is no ice in the pipes.
