If you bought or built a house in the Ķekava area up to shell finish, the pipes are already in the walls and floor, but two things remain to be settled before the day you move in: what the building regulations require before the house can be accepted for use, and whether water from the network may be used in the house.

What the building regulations require of a house with one or two flats

“Shell finish” is not a legal term but a market word for a house without interior finishes. The building regulations in general allow a building to be accepted for use even if the sanitary rooms and the kitchen are not fitted out and the interior finish is not complete. A residential house with one or two flats is an exception.

Such a house must have the general construction work and the engineering networks finished, and at least one living room, the kitchen and a sanitary unit fitted out, with the sanitary fixtures connected to the engineering networks. In practice this means that at least one bathroom or WC and the kitchen sink must be connected before the house can be accepted for use.

Cabinet Regulation No. 529 of 2 September 2014 “Ēku būvnoteikumi” (building regulations for buildings), points 164 and 184 (consolidated version on likumi.lv), checked on 24 November 2026. If the status of the house is unclear, it can be seen in the construction case file.

The meter and mains water before handover

If the house is connected to the SIA “Ķekavas nami” network, the meter cannot be taken for granted. Point 17 of Ķekava Municipality binding regulations No. 21/2022 says: if the connection was built to the technical conditions without unapproved deviations, the service provider installs a verified meter in the meter assembly built by the owner, seals it and draws up a report. If the assembly does not comply, the meter is not fitted, and the assembly must first be rebuilt.

The second point applies directly to shell finish. Point 21 says that water from the new connection may be used before the building is accepted for use only with the service provider’s written consent and through a meter it has installed. If the house was bought from a developer, ask for this consent or the report together with the other documents.

Ķekava Municipality binding regulations No. 21/2022, points 17 and 21; likumi.lv, checked on 24 November 2026. In the part of the municipality where services are provided by SIA “Rīgas ūdens”, the procedure is set by the contract with that company.

If the new house stays on a septic tank or a treatment plant

Not every new build in the Ķekava area has a network in the street. Then the house’s waste pipe goes to a septic tank, a holding tank or a biological treatment plant, and it must be in the Ķekava Municipality register of decentralised sewage systems. If the property is transferred or the system changes, for example the number of residents changes or the house is later connected to the network, the owner must report this within one month at the latest.

The plumber’s work here is on the house side: the waste pipes from the fixtures to the exit through the foundations, and the inspection points through which they can be cleaned; the treatment system itself is installed by its supplier.

The washing machine spot in shell finish

In a new build it is worth deciding on the washing machine spot before the rooms are finally divided. For example, the Swedish industry rules Säker Vatteninstallation 2026:1 say that a washing machine must stand in a room whose floor is waterproof and has a drain in it, and that the water supply to the machine needs a shut-off valve that is clearly visible and easy to reach.

That is not a Latvian rule, but it shows well why a machine should not go in a cupboard in the hallway, where there is neither a drain nor waterproofing. If the machine outlet the builder left is in a room without a drain, it can be moved, or a drain can be fitted in the room, while the floor is not yet finished. Connecting the machine to an existing tap and drain is a separate price line.

The water heater in the utility room

An electric water heater is hung on a finished wall and connected to cold and hot water with a safety valve. If the house has a heating boiler or a heat pump with an indirect hot-water cylinder, the cylinder must be connected to the heating circuit, and that is another job with its own price. The electricity supply to the water heater and the water heater itself are not in the plumber’s price.

Connecting fixtures to ready outlets

There is no single price for finishing on ready outlets, because houses differ in the number of fixtures and the state of the outlets. The sum is set by the number of fixtures, whether the outlets match the plan and have been pressure-tested, and whether a water heater and manifold have to be connected too. Individual fixtures have starting prices: one bathroom fixture from €60, a floor-standing WC from €60, a concealed frame with a wall-hung WC from €85, connecting an electric water heater from €80, a washing machine hook-up from €35. Fixtures and materials are not included in the price. If an outlet is not where the chosen fixture will stand, moving it has its own page, moving waste outlets.

Questions: Finishing plumbing in a new build

We are moving in, but the house has not yet been accepted for use. May we use water from the network?

Only with the service provider’s written consent and through a meter it has installed, as point 21 of binding regulations No. 21/2022 says. If there is no such consent, first contact SIA “Ķekavas nami”, and only then connect the fixtures for use.

Can a private house be accepted for use if the bathroom has no tiles yet?

For a house with one or two flats, the building regulations require a fitted-out sanitary unit with fixtures connected to the engineering networks, but the regulations say nothing about tiles. What counts is connected fixtures, not the finish. How it is assessed in a particular case is decided by the building authority.

The house stood unheated all winter with water in the pipes. Can the fixtures be connected straight away?

The system must first be pressure-tested, because frozen water in a pipe or joint can cause a crack that only shows when there is water in the pipes again. If the test passes, the fixtures can be connected; if not, the damaged spot is found and replaced before the finishes.
