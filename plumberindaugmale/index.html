Daugmale is the centre of Daugmale parish in Ķekava Municipality, and the village is in the Ķekavas nami tariff zone. Around it are two other settings: blocks on both sides of the P85 and new areas with detailed plans.

“Salnas”: an application and a water sample in one building

The Ķekavas nami customer service centre is also in Daugmale, at “Salnas”, and an application for technical conditions for a connection is accepted there. The form is on the Ķekavas nami water management page, the company also accepts electronically signed applications, and a copy of the land register deed is attached. Once the technical conditions are received, the owner builds the service pipe according to them, has it surveyed after the work, and draws up a completion certificate with the company’s representative (binding regulations No. 21/2022, point 15).

In the same building on 6 May 2026 a water sample was taken after the hydrophore had been cleaned and disinfected. Total hardness was 3.12 mmol/L, iron below 0.04 mg/L, pH 7.5. This is a sample from one tap on one day, not a description of the whole village’s water supply, but hardness of about 3 mmol/L means limescale in a water heater: routine water-heater service costs from €75 per heater.

Collective-farm blocks on both sides of the P85 and houses along Ciemata ceļš

After the Second World War Daugmale was the centre of the “Sarkanais strēlnieks” collective farm, later the parish centre. It is characterised by multi-storey blocks in the centre and individual houses along the old road to the former ferry crossing. In the village assessment (2019), dense development with blocks of flats and detached houses is described on both sides of the P85, with detached houses also along Ciemata ceļš, but within the village boundaries approved in 2007 there is also extensive farmland and forest.

In a block of flats the fault may lie not only in the flat’s own pipes: if a water riser or soil stack leaks, the stretch in the flat is replaced in agreement with the building manager, because a riser is usually common property. A new water riser stretch from floor to ceiling costs from €110 per storey, replacing a cast-iron soil stack stretch with plastic from €100 per storey; more on the work on the page riser replacement.

For a detached house along Ciemata ceļš or Vedmeru, Raģu and Pikstu ceļš it helps the plumber to know the road name, because the basis of the street network in Daugmale is the P85, the V9 (Iecava-Baldone-Daugmale) and municipal roads. If a house has its own well but wants to send its wastewater into the Ķekavas nami sewer, there is a separate requirement (see the questions below).

More than twenty detailed plans and “Meža Sētas”

One local plan and more than twenty detailed plans have been drawn up in Daugmale, mainly for dividing properties for residential development, and most of the village is designated as an area requiring a detailed plan. For a new house it is therefore necessary first to know what the plan of your own plot says about water and waste.

The local plan “Meža Sētas” (SN-TPD-8/2026, approved on 20 August 2026) requires connection to the centralised water supply and sewer as soon as they are built up to the area, and until then provides biological treatment units with an infiltration system. Once a house is joined to the network, point 16 of Ķekava Municipality binding regulations No. 21/2022 requires decommissioning wastewater collection structures previously used on the plot that may harm the environment. Whether this applies to your BIO unit and how to disconnect it is worth finding out when you receive the technical conditions.

For a house in such an area, the position of the outlet from the building is set by where on the plot the BIO unit will stand; laying internal runs to the fixtures starts from €55 per connection point, and the work is described on the page first-fix plumbing.

Questions from Daugmale about water and connection

In Daugmale a new house has a BIO unit until the network is built. What happens to it when the network reaches the plot?

In the “Meža Sētas” area the local plan requires connection as soon as the networks are built up to the area, and the procedure starts with an application for technical conditions to Ķekavas nami. After connection, Ķekava Municipality regulations No. 21/2022 (point 16) require decommissioning old collection structures that may harm the environment, so what will happen to the BIO unit is worth finding out already in the service pipe design.

The Daugmale water sample was taken after the hydrophore was cleaned. Does it describe the water in my tap too?

In part. The sample shows the network water at one tap at “Salnas” on 6 May 2026: hardness 3.12 mmol/L, iron below 0.04 mg/L, pH 7.5. At your tap the water still passes through the house’s pipes and water heater, and the sample does not measure those. House pipes usually do not change hardness, so limescale builds up in a water heater all the same.

An old house in Daugmale has its own well. Can it connect only to the Ķekavas nami sewer?

The regulations allow it. If a house uses its own water source but sends wastewater into the centralised sewer, point 59 of Ķekava Municipality binding regulations No. 21/2022 requires submitting to the service provider the technical documentation, the source diagram and the pump documentation. How the water discharged is metered is specified in the technical conditions.
