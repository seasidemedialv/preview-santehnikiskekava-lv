When a summer house starts to be lived in all year round, the first problem is usually the supply line, which was meant for summer and freezes in winter.

From the Jenču pļavas meadows to houses lived in over winter

Jenči, earlier Jenču pļavas, is a village in the south-east of Ķekava parish on the right bank of the Ķekaviņa, with an area of 0.51 km². In the village assessment of the territorial plan (2019), the buildings are both summer houses and garden houses and detached houses. To the south-west and south Jenči borders the Baldone side, to the east it is surrounded by forest, and to the north by farmland. There are no public or municipal facilities in the village.

In a garden house built for summer, the plumbing can be simple: a pipe in shallow ground or above ground, a tap in the garden, a sink or a WC with waste to a pit or a tank. In autumn the water is drained, in spring it is started. In a house where people live in January such a system does not work, and it is rebuilt in stages: supply line, internal pipes, hot water, waste.

What changes so that a garden house has water in winter too

The first is the supply line: the pipe from the well or borehole to the house has to run below the frost depth or be heated and insulated, and where it enters the house there must be no cold gaps. The second is the internal pipes: they have to run through heated rooms, not under the floor of a ventilated crawl space or in an outer wall. The third is the water heater, whose volume was enough for summer weekends but may be too small for a family all year. We describe this whole rebuild on the page garden house for year-round use; its price is set by the depth and length of the supply line, the heating cable and insulation, the internal waste and the water heater.

If the supply line has already frozen, the job is thawing frozen pipes and checking for a split. When the ice has melted, a crack can show up as a dripping joint or a wet spot, and locating and sealing it costs from €35 per spot. An old pipe inside the house is replaced with a new one: running new water pipe in place of the old one from €10/m. If the house is nevertheless left empty in winter, draining in autumn and starting in spring suits.

The garden tap is another place to rethink in a rebuild. In a summer house it can be on a pipe that is drained in winter together with everything else. In a house lived in all year, the garden tap needs its own shut-off inside the house, so that in autumn only the outdoor section can be closed and drained. The price of a garden tap with a winter shut-off is set by the wall material and thickness, the length of the supply to the tap and whether a frost-proof tap is needed.

Before a rebuild it is worth knowing one more thing. Building in Jenči is restricted by the 10% flood probability risk area set for the Ķekava river. If the rebuild means an extension or a new building, first find out whether your plot is in this area, because it may affect what may be built and where.

The cooperative's streets and the holding tank in the yard

The whole street network of the garden cooperative in Jenči has passed into municipal ownership, but not all the streets have been built. If your street exists only on paper, write in the request from which side it can be reached. Jenči is not on the village list of the SIA “Ķekavas nami” tariff zones, so for every house we first ask about the source of its water and the outlet for its waste.

If the waste goes to a holding tank, the Ķekava Municipality regulations set the minimum emptying frequency with the formula I = B/A. B is the monthly water use of the people living in the house, taking 1.5 m³ per person, or the actual use by the meter; A is the tank volume in cubic metres. If I is below 1, the tank is emptied less often than once a month, and then the number of times a year is calculated: Ig = M × I, where M is the number of months the house is used.

This is exactly where the move to year-round living shows in numbers. For two people B = 3 m³ per month. With a 6 m³ tank I = 0.5. If the house is used for four summer months, Ig = 4 × 0.5 = 2 times a year; if for all twelve, Ig = 12 × 0.5 = 6 times a year.

Ķekava Municipality binding regulations No. 20/2022, point 7 (the holding tank emptying frequency formula), likumi.lv, checked on 24 November 2026.

Questions in Jenči

A garden house in Jenči has a holding tank: how many times must it be emptied if we now live there all year?

By the formula in point 7 of Ķekava Municipality regulations No. 20/2022: the number of people times 1.5 m³ a month (or the use by the meter), divided by the tank volume. This gives times per month. If the result is below 1, it is multiplied by the number of months of use, and for year-round living that is 12.

Jenči or Jeņči: how should the address be written?

The official name is Jenči, Ķekava parish. The word Jeņči, like Jenču pļavas, is an earlier name, and it can be seen in old papers. Either will do in the form, if you add the street and the house number.

We are converting a summer house in Jenči into a house: what in the plumbing should be changed first?

The supply line, because it decides whether there will be water in winter at all. After that the pipes that run through unheated places, and only then the water heater and the internal finish. For the sewage, first assess whether the existing pit or tank suits permanent living.
