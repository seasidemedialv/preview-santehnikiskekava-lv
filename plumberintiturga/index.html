That means the blocks' water and sewer risers date from one time, unless some block or flat has replaced them. Titurga is a part of the town of Baloži in Ķekava Municipality, not a separate village in Ķekava parish.

An estate built in one go

Titurga was designed and built as a complex residential estate, and in 1988 it was awarded the USSR State Prize. Not everything designed was built: the planned secondary school and kindergarten were left unbuilt. The flats, the boiler house and the treatment works were completed, though, and so the blocks of Titurga are likely to be more alike than houses in places that grew gradually.

For a plumber this has a convenient side and an inconvenient one. The convenient one: if it is known how the risers are laid out in one block, a similar picture can be expected in the next block. The inconvenient one: the pipes in all the blocks are of one age, so if a riser has started to leak in one flat, the neighbours on the same vertical have sections just as old. Water and sewerage services in Baloži, including Titurga, are provided by SIA “Ķekavas nami” from 1 September 2026.

What changes in a 1980s block

A water riser is usually noticed first when rust or damp appears at the joints. Replacing risers costs from €110 per storey: a new section of cold or hot water riser in one flat from floor to ceiling, with transitions to the neighbouring sections. For a Ø110 soil stack, replacing a cast-iron section with plastic costs from €100 per storey. In both cases agreeing it with the building manager and materials are extra.

In a cast-iron soil stack the new plastic section in one flat joins the neighbours' cast-iron sections above and below, and the transitions are the most sensitive part of the job. So it helps the building manager and us to know which riser it is (kitchen or bathroom), which floor the flat is on and whether the neighbours above or below you have already replaced their section. The riser is shut off for the whole vertical while the work is done, so the time is agreed with the building manager.

The horizontal kitchen and bathroom outlets up to the riser are usually part of the flat, unless the building’s documents say otherwise. If a kitchen is being rebuilt, it is worth replacing the outlet to the riser while the new cabinets are not yet fitted. If a kitchen sink drains slowly or smells, the old outlet may have grown clogged, and sewer pipe replacement costs from €30 for a waste pipe to one fixture. If the water simply does not go away, first see the guide water will not drain.

If a building wants to replace a whole vertical or all the risers at once, it is organised by the building manager or the flat owners' association. Such work has a separate page for building managers.

Lake Titurga, the treatment works and what may go down the drain

Lake Titurga is an overgrowing flow-through brown-water lake on the eastern edge of the Medema bog, with low, peaty shores and a 3.5–4.0 m layer of sapropel. When the treatment works were finished at the end of the 1980s, the lake began to be cleaned too, and silt and sapropel were pumped out of it. The Titurga river flows out of the lake, regulated along almost its whole length as a ditch about 8 m wide, and runs into the Sausā Daugava near Alejas.

The estate has had its own wastewater treatment from the start, and the network is meant only for domestic wastewater. Ķekava Municipality binding regulations No. 21/2022 forbid sending rain and snow water, drainage water, solids, fats and floating insoluble parts larger than 4 mm into the sewer (point 49). They also set limits for the wastewater itself: no hotter than +40 °C and with a pH from 6.5 to 8.5 (point 44.4). In a block of flats this is a practical rule: fat poured out in the kitchen cools in the shared riser and narrows it for everyone living lower down. The same rule applies to the detached houses around the blocks: rain water from a roof and drainage water must not get into the domestic sewer.

What is forbidden in the domestic sewer and the wastewater temperature: regulations No. 21/2022, points 49 and 44.4, likumi.lv/ta/id/338066; viewed on 23 November 2026.

Questions about block pipes in Titurga

In documents is Titurga in Baloži or in Ķekava parish?

Baloži. Titurga is a part of the town of Baloži, and Baloži is a town in Ķekava Municipality, not part of the parish. Water and sewerage up to the block are provided from 1 September 2026 by SIA “Ķekavas nami”, to which SIA “Baložu komunālā saimniecība” was added, and Baloži has its own tariff zone.

In a Titurga block we want to pour boiling water from a pot into the sink. Do the rules allow it?

Point 44.4 of Ķekava Municipality binding regulations No. 21/2022 says wastewater must not be hotter than +40 °C and its pH must be from 6.5 to 8.5. So boiling water from a pot is best left to cool or poured together with cold water from the tap. Fat and oil are not poured down the drain at any temperature (point 49), because in a shared riser they cool and narrow the pipe for those living lower down.

The Titurga blocks were built at one time. Does that mean they all have the same pipes?

Originally they were similar, because the estate was built to one design by the end of the 1980s. Since then the pipes in flats and blocks may have been replaced, so one vertical can hold both cast iron and plastic. Before the work it is worth looking at what is in your flat and with the neighbours above and below you, because the transitions to the neighbouring sections depend on it.
